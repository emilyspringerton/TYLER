## II. THE ARITHMETIC (the only part everyone agrees on)

```
gregorian_months_days = [31, 28, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31, 31, 30, 31, 30, 31]
total_greg_days   = 365
fixed_calendar    = 13 * 28 = 364
day_out_of_time   = 365 - 364 = 1
```

Twelve months of uneven length add to 365 days. Thirteen months of exactly four weeks add to 364.
**One day belongs to neither grid.** The transcript calls it the *Day Out of Time*: a day with no
month and no day of the week.[^1]

## III. THE CLAIMS (as transcribed)

1. The extra day was a "universal, boundaryless reset," a blank cell in which nothing is recorded.
2. The 12-month calendar was built so that no such cell survives: its holidays are bound to weekdays,
   and so to payroll.
3. "No rest for the wicked" is therefore a standing instruction: *if you twist the grid, you do not
   get the blank cell.*[^2]
4. The counter-move is **private**: perform the schedule outwardly, keep your own unlogged day
   inside it.

## IV. THE CATALOGUER'S FOOTNOTES

[^1]: The arithmetic is correct and the transcript's own check returns it. It is incomplete: in a
leap year the Gregorian total is **366**, the gap is **two** days, and the transcript never says so.
The archive notes two unowned days in those years. It declines to say which of them is the
transcript's "blank cell."

[^2]: The etymology offered for "wicked" (a shared root with *wicca*, *wicker*, "to bend") is
asserted, not established, and philologists dispute it. The archive keeps the claim because the
**Jiangshi Syndicate's own production notes use the same slogan**, unprompted, on Day 31. The
Cataloguer cannot destroy a record that endangers it. The footnote is, as always, personal.

**What the Cataloguer will say plainly:** the transcript's villain is a single scheduling
conspiracy. The archive finds nothing to support a single author of any calendar. It finds only
that one of Tyler's files has a date field that has been empty since Build 0015, and that the
Shell Parliament's accrual engine cannot divide by it.
