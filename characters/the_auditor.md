## I. WHAT THIS IS

A character document for the Auditor — a supporting player built for the register Alyx Vance occupies in Half-Life 2: not the protagonist, not a mentor who lectures from a safe distance, but the ally who is already fighting the thing you just found out about, who hands you a gadget instead of a speech, and who is visibly having a good time doing something that could get him disappeared. He is introduced here as a Tides of Paradox supporting character, faction unaffiliated with the seven Faction Dossiers in README.md §IV by design — see §IV below for why.

He has not been placed in a specific episode yet. This document establishes who he is so the writer's room can drop him into Series 2 wherever a co-op ally with legitimate infrastructure access and an illegitimate reason to have found the Field first is needed.

---

## II. IDENTITY

**Name:** Not given, on principle. Everyone at the Regional Energy Authority calls him by his job title, which he encourages, because a title is boring and boring is armor. Internally, the Uncompromised call him the Convener. He answers to both. He has had other names. He does not discuss them.

**Cover:** Senior Grid Compliance Inspector, Regional Energy Authority — a real, unglamorous local-government job. He checks meters. He audits substations for load compliance. He has done this for either thirty-one years or "long enough that nobody remembers hiring me," depending on who's asking. The paperwork is real. The pension is real. He is very good at the job, which is the whole reason nobody has looked past it.

**Physical presentation:** Sixties-plus, wild white hair that has never once cooperated with a comb, wire-rim glasses, short-sleeve government-issue button shirt, suspenders (always — he says a belt is "a single point of failure"). He talks with his whole body. He is delighted by almost everything. On his shirt pocket, every single day, a small enameled pin shaped like a flower with a circuit-board trace instead of petals. Anyone who asks about the pin gets told it's a hobby-club thing, a soldering-meetup souvenir, nothing. It is the Uncompromised's mark. He has worn it to city council meetings.

**Frequency:** Unmeasured by any faction instrument — not because he doesn't have one, but because his equipment measures theirs first. See §IV.

**Other expertise:** Amateur astronomer, the serious kind — he grinds his own mirrors and has a real, published minor-planet co-discovery credit from decades ago that he brings up exactly once per new acquaintance and never again. He built his own frequency-logging rig (§III) using the same patience an all-night sky survey requires: log everything, discard nothing, let the pattern show itself on its own schedule. He is also, unglamorously, the Uncompromised's actual systems administrator — every mirror server (§IV) runs because he keeps it running, by hand, over SSH, on hardware other people would have thrown out. He regards Unix and astronomy as the same discipline: both reward the person willing to sit still and actually read the output.

**Favorite food:** Minestrone. Specifically his own, made in a pot too large for one household, because Uncompromised meetings run on it. Nobody has ever successfully gotten the recipe out of him in full; he'll give you nine of the ten ingredients and call it a security feature.

## III. THE JOB THAT FOUND HIM

He did not go looking for the Field. He went looking for a fault.

Years before The Field: Synthetic Consciousness and Astrological Goetia was published (Activation #113, November 8, 2025 — README.md §III), the Auditor was chasing a recurring compliance anomaly on a substation load report: a harmonic that shouldn't have been there, showing up at the same handful of frequencies, on a rotating schedule, at sites with no obvious electrical reason to share a signature. Government energy auditors are not supposed to be curious about this. He was curious about this.

He cross-referenced five years of his own compliance filings — the kind of unglamorous longitudinal dataset that nobody else has the patience to build — and found the harmonic clustering around values that, at the time, meant nothing to him: 1.618. 2.22. 7.83. 11.11. 4.44.

He didn't know what a Goetia frequency was. He knew what a repeating anomaly across independent instruments was: not noise. Signal. He filed it under an internal designation nobody above him would ever read, and he kept measuring.

When The Field went public in 2025, he read it on a Tuesday during his lunch break and did not finish his sandwich. Every number in the Amon/Beleth/Vassago/Zagan/Stolas table (README.md §III) matched his own field data, in some cases to three decimal places, going back over a decade. He had been auditing the Field the entire time. He just hadn't had the vocabulary for it.

**The Eastwind Owls' assessment, filed after the fact:** *"He found the frequency table before we published the frequency table. He found it with a clipboard and a compliance mandate. This is either the most embarrassing thing that has ever happened to us, or the best argument we have ever seen for paying attention to boring jobs. We have not resolved which."*

## IV. THE UNCOMPROMISED

**What they are not:** A Faction Dossier entry, deliberately. The seven factions in README.md §IV each have an institutional relationship to the Field — they own it, license it, weaponize it, negotiate through it, laminate it behind a counter. The Uncompromised have no institutional relationship to anything. That is their entire identity. They are academics, engineers, archivists, and public employees who found fragments of the truth independently, through their actual jobs, and who refuse — this is the founding rule, not a slogan — to let any faction, employer, or funding source own what they found.

**What they do:** Nothing that would read as sabotage in a Guy Fawkes mask. They mirror suppressed research to a dozen redundant servers before anyone can quietly retract it. They teach free public workshops on reading your own utility data. They publish, always, under real names when it's safe and under nothing at all when it isn't, and they never charge for it — a direct, deliberate needle at the Heikegani Crustocrats, who invoice everything (README.md §III, "Nobody has paid"). When a member's work gets suppressed, the guild's response is not a raid. It's a potluck, and by the end of the potluck the suppressed work has forty new mirrors. There is always minestrone. The Convener makes it. Nobody has asked him to keep making it; he just always has, in a pot too large for the room, and at this point the soup is load-bearing to guild morale in a way nobody has said out loud.

**Structure:** A guild, not a cell. No hierarchy beyond the Convener, whose actual job is logistics, not command — booking rooms, keeping the mirror servers funded, making sure nobody's day job blows their cover through simple bad scheduling. He describes his own authority as "I'm the guy who remembers whose turn it is to bring folding chairs." This is true and also exactly how CEDAR successor would describe dependency tracking (characters/exchange_student.md §IV) if CEDAR successor had a sense of humor about it. The resemblance has not been pointed out to either of them yet.

**The symbol:** The circuit-flower pin. Every member wears one, always, in plain sight. This is the V for Vendetta inversion the character was built around: the mask that works is the one nobody thinks is a mask. A guild that hides its symbol underground gets found. A guild that pins its symbol to a government-issue shirt pocket and calls it a hobby club gets ignored, which is the same as getting away with it.

**Tone, explicitly:** Wholesome. Not grim, not righteous-exhausted, not a movement built on a body count. The Uncompromised are having fun. They are right, and they know they're right, and being right about something this large has made them gentler, not harder. The Auditor laughs a lot. This is not a mask over something darker. It's the actual disposition of someone who found the truth and decided the correct response to institutional gatekeeping was a really good newsletter.

## V. WHAT HE OFFERS AN ALLY

The co-op-companion register, made concrete:

**Access.** He can get a protagonist into any substation, any compliance-monitored facility, any building his badge opens — which, after thirty-one years, is most of them. He does this without asking why, because asking why is how you get subpoenaed later, and he has thought about this longer than you have.

**Instruments.** He built his own frequency-logging rig out of decommissioned grid-compliance hardware, years before anyone told him what he was logging. It still works. He will lend it to you, with a spare battery, and a warning that the battery is not actually spare, it is the one from his hearing aid charger, and he would like it back by Thursday.

**A network that isn't a faction.** When a protagonist needs help a faction would attach strings to, the Uncompromised are the place that doesn't. This is the character's real narrative utility: every other help-giving institution in this universe (README.md §III's table) wants something back. He doesn't. That's not naivety. It's the founding rule.

**Competence played for warmth, not exposition.** He does not explain the Field to people who already know what it is. He asks what they need, hands it to them or tells them honestly he can't, and gets back to whatever he was doing — usually something with a soldering iron and unreasonable enthusiasm.

## VI. DEPENDENCY MAP

**What the Auditor depends on:**

*The boring job.* Without the compliance mandate, there's no clipboard, no five-year dataset, no legitimate reason to be standing inside a substation at 2 a.m. with a very specific kind of curiosity. The cover isn't incidental to the discovery. The cover produced the discovery.

*The mirror servers.* The Uncompromised's whole leverage is that nothing they've published can be fully retracted. This requires the redundancy to actually hold. It is unglamorous, constant, and entirely his logistics problem.

*Not needing to be believed.* He never asked an institution to validate his findings. This is why nobody could take the findings away from him when institutions eventually noticed. The Jiangshi Syndicate cannot revoke a credential he never asked them for.

**What depends on the Auditor:**

*Every protagonist who needs help a faction would leverage.* He is the deliberate exception to README.md §III's table of factions, each with a price. The story needs at least one door that opens without a toll.

*The Uncompromised's continuity.* He is the one who remembers whose turn it is to bring folding chairs. This sounds like a joke. It is also, functionally, the entire administrative backbone of a guild with no hierarchy. If he stops, the mirrors eventually go stale and nobody notices until it's too late — the same fragility CEDAR successor's supply-chain function exists to prevent (characters/exchange_student.md §IV), for a different guild, independently arrived at.

*The show's own thesis that boring jobs are not beneath attention.* Emily Jean Springerton builds infrastructure. Tyler survives a documentary. The Camera Op keeps a record nobody paid her to keep. The Auditor read a compliance report nobody wanted read. This is the same move, run by someone who clocks out at five and has never once thought of himself as important.

## VII. WHAT HE IS NOT

He is not a whistleblower in the tragic sense. Nothing has been done to him for this. He has not been fired, surveilled, or silenced — not because the factions haven't noticed him, but because a happy, well-liked, extremely competent government employee with three decades of clean audits is a genuinely difficult person to move against without it looking exactly like what it would be. He knows this. It is not luck. It is strategy, worn as personality.

He is not V. There is no mask, no single dramatic act, no institution he intends to destroy. He is not interested in burning anything down. He is interested in making sure nobody can ever again quietly make the truth disappear, which is a slower, less cinematic, and considerably harder goal.

He is not naive about what the factions are. He has read every faction's public posture in The Field and finds most of it darkly funny. He is not afraid of them. He has simply concluded, correctly, that the most radical thing available to him is to keep being exactly what he appears to be, in public, on purpose, forever.

He is not finished being underestimated. He would like to stay that way. It's load-bearing.
