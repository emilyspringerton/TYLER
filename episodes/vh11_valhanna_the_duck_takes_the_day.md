# TYLER — VALHANNA, EPISODE 11 — COLD OPEN
## "Le Canard Prend la Journée" ("The Duck Takes the Day")
### Build 0149 | Einhorn Media | VALHANNA, continued

---

**PRODUCTION NOTE, READ BEFORE THE SCRIPT:**

VH01 framed the whole opening as the Duck's dream. VH03 opened on the Duck waking to a
thirteen-page calendar and closing with "That's not how the month goes." Since then the Duck has
been absent from the crew's room. VH11 is the Duck's own account of the Day Out of Time, narrated
secondhand, in old-noir affectation — the exact register of Season 6 (Take 2) E2, "Yellow, Cut
Short" (Build 0138, founder direction: "the duck from redgarden," "old noire style").

**The Duck is an unreliable narrator by its own admission.** It says so in the first line. Its
account is embellished and Tyler is not here to correct the record. The cold open takes the
absence as part of the joke.

**Language:** English. The Duck has not yet regained any French (see VH22).

**Not resolved:** how much of the Duck's account is true; Hana; the Bird Correction (PENDING).

## COLD OPEN

```
INT. THE DUCK'S APARTMENT — NIGHT — BLACK AND WHITE
```

*Venetian blinds throw slats across a table. A desk lamp. A single glass with an inch of something
in it. On the wall behind it, a rectangle of cleaner plaster the size of thirteen pages, and a nail with
nothing on it; the calendar lies on the table under the lamp. A folding chair is propped against the
wall, closed. The Duck sits in a chair turned backward, tuxedo collar up, a cigarette — a short straw — between its bill.*

**LOWER THIRD (DOC UI):**
```
DOCUMENTARY UNIT B // NO CREDIT // TC — / —
```

**THE DUCK (V.O.)**
It was a Tuesday. It was always a Tuesday, in this town. And I'll tell you right now, so you can
walk out while the lights are still low: I wasn't there for most of what I'm about to tell you.
I got it secondhand. From a crew with a clipboard.

*The Duck takes the straw out of its bill, examines it, puts it back.*

**THE DUCK (V.O., CONT'D)**
The facts, such as they are. Twelve months. Thirteen moons. One day over. I did the math on a
napkin. I did it twice. The napkin was a coaster. The second time it was a different coaster.

*The Duck stands. Picks the calendar up off the table and turns it to the camera. A grid
of twenty-eight squares, four rows of seven. It holds the page up to the lens, like a mugshot.*

**THE DUCK (V.O., CONT'D)**
Twenty-eight. Four weeks. A tidy little month. Not a day hanging off the end. And somebody — I'm
not going to say who, I'm a duck, I get sued — somebody *kept* the day. Put it in a drawer. Left
it out of the books. Left it for a rainy one.

*It lays the calendar back on the table. It's crooked. It squares it to the edge. It does not hang it
back on the nail. The nail is not its to use.*

---

*A flash cut, in the Duck's telling: THE CREW AS NOIR FIGURES. Cam in a trench coat with a camera
instead of a gun, at the end of a long hall. The PA as a man in a hat holding a clipboard like
a ledger. The sound guy as a long-armed silhouette with a boom like a fishing rod. The Producer,
off-screen, a voice only: a cigar-smoker's voice.*

**THE DUCK (V.O.)**
They were four: a shooter, a clerk, a listener, and a voice on the radio who never once said
anything he'd have to testify to.

*The Producer's voice in the Duck's version:*

**PRODUCER (O.S., the Duck's version, gravel)**
Keep rolling, kid.

**THE DUCK (V.O.)**
That's not what he said. I'm embellishing. He said "Please continue." Very polite. Very dull.

---

*Back in the apartment. The Duck pours a finger of the something. Doesn't drink it.*

**THE DUCK (V.O.)**
There was a door. A door with a light behind it. I'm told it opened onto a day nobody owned. That's
a real estate pitch if I ever heard one. Free and clear. No lien. No one's name on the deed.

*It sets the glass down, loud on the table. It glances at the folded chair against the wall and
then away from it.*

**THE DUCK (V.O., CONT'D)**
And the first one through it was an inspector with no name on his badge, a soup pot wider than the
frame, and seven folding chairs. Friendly. In my line of work that's the part you don't trust. I
had one of his chairs at the foot of my bed by morning. I told it it wasn't my turn. It agreed. It
is still there.

**THE DUCK (V.O., CONT'D)**
And you want to know the odd part? Nobody went in. Four grown professionals. A rule made of tape.
And then — I'm told — a horse walked through it like it was a wet paper bag.

*The Duck laughs once, a short quack with no mirth in it.*

**THE DUCK (V.O., CONT'D)**
I like the horse. The horse had the right idea.

---

*Close on the Duck's face as the lamp flickers. The V.O. drops its affectation, a half-tone, as if
the Duck has forgotten the voice for a second and is speaking as itself.*

**THE DUCK (V.O.)**
I have to tell you the thing I haven't told anybody. Every time I wake up in this thing, there's
less of me that remembers how I got here. It's not the French. I've made my peace with the French.
It's the part before the French.

*It looks directly into the lens. The only time in the series it has.*

**THE DUCK**
*(on camera, English, quietly)*
Whoever's watching. Make sure the tape holds.

*The lamp goes out.*

**LOWER THIRD (DOC UI), final card:**
```
NARRATOR: THE DUCK
RELIABILITY: SELF-DECLARED, LOW
THE LAST LINE WAS NOT IN THE SCRIPT
```

*SMASH TO BLACK.*

*END COLD OPEN.*
