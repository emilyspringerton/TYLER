**PRODUCTION NOTE, READ BEFORE THE SCRIPT:**

Sunday, the third. The Auditor comes with the pot. For the first time in this series, the unit is
ready for him, and what it is ready with is a chair.

`characters/the_auditor.md` §IV: *"I'm the guy who remembers whose turn it is to bring folding
chairs."* VH04: *"Seated: 3. The Auditor did not sit. A man who brings the chairs does not, by long
habit, count himself among the ones that need them"* (the Cataloguer's footnote). VH36: *"Convener, sit."
— "Sunday."* This is Sunday.

**What it is.** The tenth ingredient is given, in the open, at the table, with the lens running,
**because it is Green.** He asked on the first day that it not be done in front of the lens (VH25);
the unit has asked, since then, whether he would still ask. He does not. The word that is given is
spoken, and the audio goes to room tone, as every name does in this show. **The name is never
written** (VH25: *"A name said is a gift. A name written is a deed."*). The Auditor's badge field
stays blank, and so does this script.

**What the audience learns** (rather than what it is told): the tenth ingredient was a name. Tyler
added it on the unowned day from "later" and again on Day 8 behind a black strip of tape. **It
was always going to be the Convener's own.**

**Emily OS** never appears. The room's spacing softens by one degree. "Approval: one degree of
softening, barely perceptible. The cameras catch it" (`README.md` §XV). The Doc UI prints it.

**Not resolved:** what the name is (never); who else has one.

*Three raps on the air beside the corridor door. The Auditor comes in with the pot, as he has on the
two Sundays before, bulky and delighted, and the pot is wider than the doorway and comes through the
doorway. He is already saying "Good morning!" when he realizes nobody has said it back.*

*The PA is standing in his way. Not rudely. He has the stance of a man who has rehearsed.*

**PA**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Not today.")*
Pas aujourd'hui.

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French, gender wrong; SUBTITLED: "I beg your pardon?")*
Je vous demande pardon ?

**PA**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "The pot. Put it down.")*
La marmite. Posez-la.

*The Auditor puts the pot down, in the doorway, because it is the only place he has. He looks past the
PA into the room.*

*The tubes. The long table: the upturned pot with a cloth on it, now two cloths, a clean one on a clean one.
Eight places set, the number the unit has become, and in front of the sixth chair a bowl and a spoon and a
napkin folded in a fan, the napkin folded correctly. The sixth chair is
empty. The Duck is standing beside it, at attention, in a tuxedo, with its wings at its sides.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED, slowly: "What is this?")*
C'est quoi, ça ?

**HANA**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Sunday.")*
Dimanche.

*He does not move. He is still holding the ladle. He looks at the sixth chair, which is his own. It is
the chair he set at the start. He has set it every time. He has never sat in it.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED, with a small, startled laugh: "It's mine.")*
C'est la mienne.

**DUCK**
*(English, formal)*
Take your seat, sir.

*It makes a small bow. It moves away from the chair, and with its wing, a wing that has no business doing
anything so civilized, it draws the chair back.*

---

*The Auditor stands in front of his own chair. He is, for the first time since he came through that
door, a man who does not know what to do with his hands.*

*Tyler has risen. He has come across the room without hurrying and stopped beside the chair, with one
hand on its back. He is wearing the face of someone who has done this at a great many tables in a
great many centuries and would like very much to be good at it now.*

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED, gently: "I'd rather do it myself.")*
Je préfère le faire moi-même.

*He draws the chair out. He holds it. The Auditor looks at the chair, at the hand on the back of it,
at the room. His mouth does something small and unprofessional.*

*He sits.*

*The sixth chair takes his weight. It makes a sound as it does it, a small dry creak that belongs to
a chair that has been waiting for fifteen days for the right weight to arrive. No one in the room moves.*

*It is a very small thing. The tubes seem to gain a grain of brightness. The corners of the room are

*They serve him. Cam is the first: she takes up the ladle, which is his own, borrowed by Tyler on the first
Sunday, and in her hands it looks as if it has found its proper work. She ladles. The sound guy
brings bread, a piece torn in two. The PA lays down the spoon: the tin one, bent, his own, which Tyler has taken off his own belt
and returned to him for the day without a word. The PA holds it a second longer than he needs to
before he lets go.*

**PA**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "It's yours today.")*
Elle est à vous aujourd'hui.

*The Auditor takes it. He eats, a spoonful, then a second. His eyes close.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED, quietly: "Nine.")*
Neuf.

*Hana sits down beside him. Not across: beside him. The Auditor is surrounded by the people of the table.*

**HANA**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "We're missing the last one.")*
Il nous manque le dernier.

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED, not looking up: "Yes.")*
Oui.

CAMERA 1: GREEN

*Tyler is still standing by the chair. He has not gone back to the seventh. He bends, one hand on the
back of the chair he has just drawn out, and speaks near the Auditor's ear. It is not a whisper. It is
the voice of a man saying a short thing to someone who is listening with his whole body. It is one word.*

**LOWER THIRD (DOC UI):**
```
AUDIO: 1 NAME WITHHELD (BY WHOM: SEAT 7)
```

*Room tone, 7.83 under it, the bar below the scale. For about as long as it takes to say a word.*

*The Auditor does not move. His eyes are closed. Something goes through him from the top of his head to
the floor, the way a wave goes through a field, and his hands, which have been steady on the spoon,
let it go.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED, very low, to the soup, to himself: "That's me.")*
C'est moi.

*He tastes. It is the same soup. It is the same bowl. He sits with the spoon at his lip and then
lowers it.*

**THE AUDITOR (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Ten.")*
Dix.

---

*Nobody speaks for some time. The unit has no practice at this. Cam has lowered the rig until the lens
points at the floor; she has not turned it off; it is Green. The sound guy has taken off his headphones.
The PA has gone round the table pouring water into glasses that do not need it.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED, at last, wiping the corner of one eye with the heel of his hand, laughing, a little
wetly: "I've read other people's meters for thirty-one years.")*
Je lis les compteurs des autres depuis trente et un ans.

**HANA**
*(French; SUBTITLED, gently: "Or for as long as nobody remembers.")*
Ou depuis aussi longtemps que personne ne s'en souvient.

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Ah. You've been paying attention.")*
Ah. Vous avez écouté.

*He sets the spoon down. His voice steadies as a man's does when he is about to say a thing of which
he is quite certain.*

**THE AUDITOR (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "I give nine of ten because the tenth was my name. It's always been my name. I
put everything in the pot but that. A name's a thing you can be found by.")*
Je donne neuf sur dix parce que le dixième était mon nom. Ça a toujours été mon nom. Je mets tout
dans la marmite sauf ça. Un nom, c'est ce par quoi on peut vous retrouver.

*The sound guy, very quietly, with the evidence of nine days of black tape and eight of ceiling:*

**SOUND GUY**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Someone said it on the unowned day.")*
Quelqu'un l'a dit le jour sans date.

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Yes. And again, last Sunday, while I was reading a meter for nine minutes. Or eleven.")*
Oui. Et encore dimanche dernier, pendant que je lisais un compteur neuf minutes. Ou onze.

*He does not look at Tyler. Tyler is looking at the table.*

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED, mild, to the cloth: "I never taste first. I only add.")*
Je ne goûte jamais d'abord. J'ajoute seulement.

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED, a great gentle ruefulness: "I'd hire you.")*
Je vous embaucherais.

*The PA, at the end of the table, reaches for the clipboard, turns to a page he has not shown
anyone, and holds it up to the lens. In the corner of it, in a box, in pencil, drawn on the day of the cooking
show: a numeral, and an underline. A 7.*

**PA**
*(French; SUBTITLED, softly: "I boxed it on Saturday.")*
Je l'ai encadré samedi.

**CAM**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "We didn't see anything.")*
On n'a rien vu.

**DUCK**
*(English)*
I saw a pot.

---

*Later. The pot has been emptied. The Auditor has been sitting for an hour. It is a record. He has
been given more bread than a man can eat and he has not got up, not once, to fetch anything. At the
end of the table the PA, with his pen, has leaned across.*

**PA**
*(French; SUBTITLED, offering the pen: "Would you like to write it? On the badge?")*
Voulez-vous l'écrire ? Sur le badge ?

*The lanyard is on the table: the laminated card turned a half-inch the wrong way, the way it
always is. The field that says NAME has a dash in it. The PA holds the pen out across the cloth.*

*The Auditor takes the badge in his hand and looks at the dash for some time. And then he turns the
card face down on the cloth and slides the pen back across to the PA.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED, smiling, with all of his face: "Said, it's a gift. Written, it's a deed.")*
Dit, c'est un cadeau. Écrit, c'est un acte.

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED, softly: "I read that somewhere.")*
Je l'ai lu quelque part.

*They both laugh. Hana laughs. The Duck laughs, the odd short dry sound of a bird who is in on
it. The older Hana, in the eighth chair, has her hand over her eyes, and her shoulders move.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED, to Tyler, picking up the ladle that lies by his bowl: "Keep the ladle. It
costs you nothing and you'll bring it back.")*
Gardez la louche. Ça ne vous coûte rien et vous la rapporterez.

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "I owe you a ladle.")*
Je vous dois une louche.

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED, delighted: "It is the smallest thing you can owe a man who makes soup.")*
C'est la plus petite chose qu'on puisse devoir à un homme qui fait de la soupe.
