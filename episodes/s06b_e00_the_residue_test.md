## ACT 1 — THE RESIDUE TEST

Detroit looks ordinary until the camera starts rolling.

Not cinematic-ordinary.

Municipal ordinary. Damp concrete. Sodium lights. A parking structure that smells like rain
trapped in old receipts. The documentary crew hates this kind of opening because there's "nothing
to point at," which is exactly why Hana picked it.

"Keep it wide," Hana says, not to the city but to the lens. "If it twitches, I want the
environment twitching before the people."

Tyler is already halfway under the half-open service gate, hoodie snagging on a bent strip of
metal.

"Can we not do the haunted garage thing tonight?" he says. "I'm in a real shoes mood."

The cameraman—nobody calls him by name anymore, just Cam—tilts down.

There's something on Tyler's palm.

At first it reads like spilled soda. Thick, translucent, pearly in the bad fluorescent wash. But
when Tyler wipes his hand on the concrete pillar, it doesn't smear. It strings. Like melted
plastic. Like sugar glass. Like hot glue that never got the message to cool.

Tyler stares at it.

The string between his hand and the pillar stretches to a wire-thin filament.

Then it snaps.

The filament remains hanging in the air for a beat too long before it recoils—slowly—into the
pillar.

Cam lowers the camera.

"Did that just—"

"Keep rolling," Hana says.

Her voice is too flat. That's how the crew knows she's scared.

Tyler rubs his thumb against his palm. The residue gathers, not under pressure, but under
attention, bunching toward the center of his hand as if it knows where he's looking.

"Don't touch your face," Hana says, immediate, sharp.

"Wasn't going to."

He absolutely was.

At the edge of the frame, one of the production assistants (new kid, bright sneakers, still
thinks batteries are the main thing to worry about) shines a flashlight at the pillar where Tyler
wiped his hand.

There's writing there now.

Not paint. Not marker. Not carved. Just a sheen in the concrete, same translucent film, arranged
like finger-traced letters:

DON'T PEEL IT

The assistant laughs because people laugh when the room gets smarter than them.

"Nah, who did that?"

Nobody answers, because everybody was on camera.

Hana steps in close, not touching the pillar. She crouches, eyes level with the message, and sees
the rest of it becoming legible as the light angle shifts:

DON'T PEEL IT IF YOU WANT IT TO STAY SMALL

Tyler takes a step back.

"Okay. Great. Cool. It writes. So this is some prank polymer thing. We can leave."

Hana doesn't move.

"Tyler," she says quietly, "what exactly did you touch before you came in?"

He opens his mouth to say nothing.

Then he frowns.

His expression changes in a way Cam hates filming because it always reads too real later.

Tyler turns his hand over again. The sticky sheen is now in the shape of a partial fingerprint—
except the loops and whorls aren't his.

They look like a tiny map.

"...I don't remember," he says.

And that is worse than if he had said blood.

**End of Act 1 Beat**
The crew has a physical anomaly with a rule attached. Tyler has contamination plus a memory gap.

## ACT 2 — CLEANUP FOOTAGE

They try normal things first, because people always do.

Gloves. Alcohol wipes. Hand soap from a gas station bathroom. Bottled water. A microfiber cloth.
A knife (Tyler's idea, vetoed). One of those industrial orange degreaser packets the sound guy
keeps in his bag because he treats all crises like they're food-truck adjacent.

None of it works.

The residue transfers to gloves, but never leaves skin. It beads under alcohol, then re-joins
itself. Water makes it cloudy for a second, and in that second Cam catches something in macro
that nobody notices until playback:

The clouded surface contains frames.

Tiny moving frames.

Like reflections from places they are not standing.

Hana calls an immediate stop and makes them huddle by the stairwell where the acoustics are bad
enough that even if the building is listening, it has to work for it.

"Ground rules," she says. "No one touches it bare-handed except Tyler because he's already
tagged. No peeling. No scraping. No heat. No fire."

"Why no fire?" asks the assistant.

"Because if the haunted note says don't peel, then peeling is probably a mechanical trigger. Fire
is a wildcard."

Tyler leans against the rail, trying to look bored, which is his tell for panic.

"It's glue. Evil glue."

"Sure," Hana says. "Then let's beat glue by being less stupid than glue."

The crew has seen her do this before: build a protocol while pretending they aren't in one of
those nights.

Cam rewinds the footage on the on-board monitor.

Frame by frame, Tyler's palm clouds under water. Inside the clouding, there are tiny, impossible
reflections:

a hallway with the wrong wallpaper,
a payphone glowing in daylight,
someone in a dark suit turning away from camera,
a flip phone open in a hand with chipped black nail polish.

Hana's breathing changes.

"Back up."

Cam backs up.

There it is again: the flip phone.

The image is too small to identify the screen, but everybody recognizes the silhouette because
it's so aggressively out of time it becomes iconic.

Tyler sees Hana clock it.

"You know that phone."

"No," Hana says too fast.

"Cool, so yes."

She ignores him. Points at the pillar message instead.

The words are changing.

Not sentence by sentence. Tone by tone. As if the message is revising itself to meet the room.

DON'T PEEL IT IF YOU WANT IT TO STAY SMALL

becomes

DO NOT SEPARATE LAYERS WITHOUT CONTAINMENT

then, while they watch,

WHO GAVE HIM ACCESS

The assistant makes a noise and backs into a light stand.

"Us?" Cam says, immediately hating that he said it out loud.

Tyler looks up from his hand. "Him? Who's him?"

No one answers because the sticky on Tyler's palm has started crawling up his wrist in
thread-thin veins that pause whenever he looks directly at them.

"Eyes off it," Hana says.

"What?"

"Everybody. Don't track it. Peripheral only."

"Why."

"Because it's responding to focus."

She says it like a hypothesis and a confession at the same time.

Cam, who has spent half his life behind lenses and therefore knows exactly how predatory
attention can be, lowers the camera and points it at the floor by instinct.

The crawling slows.

Tyler laughs once, brittle. "So we're getting beat by eye contact now."

The stairwell emergency light flickers.

Not the fluorescent buzz-flutter. A clean digital blink. On. Off. On.

When it comes back, there's a fresh line of residue text on the rail beside Tyler's hand.

No one saw it being written.

IT LIKES WHEN YOU CALL IT STUFF

Tyler goes very still.

"Okay," he says, voice low. "That's not funny."

"Did anyone say 'stuff'?" the assistant whispers.

Everyone turns to look at Tyler, because he did. Three minutes ago. On mic.

Tyler's jaw tightens. "Nope. I hate this."

Hana steps closer, not touching him.

"Listen to me. If this is lexical—if naming changes behavior—then we stop improvising. We use
controlled terms only. We document every phrase."

Cam raises the camera again.

Hana snaps, "No direct close-up."

He pauses. "You just said document every phrase."

"With audio. Wide visuals. No focal lock."

A beat.

Then the sound guy, of all people, mutters: "Like it sticks better when we resolve it."

Everybody turns.

He shrugs, embarrassed. "I mean... in sound if you isolate a noisy signal too hard you can end up
amplifying the wrong thing."

Hana points at him. "That. Keep doing that."

The building answers with a wet, quiet sound from somewhere below them.

Not drip. Not creak.

More like tape being pulled slowly off painted drywall.

Then Tyler winces and grips the rail.

The residue on his wrist has reached the cuff of his sleeve.

And underneath the fabric, something on his forearm starts glowing through like a phone screen
pressed against skin.

**End of Act 2 Beat**
Cleanup and naming both fail. The contamination is reactive, language-sensitive, and
attention-sensitive. Tyler is becoming an interface.

## ACT 3 — TOUCH EVENT / FIRST HALF CLIFFHANGER

They should leave.

They don't, because leaving requires agreement and fear makes committees out of people.

Hana wants a controlled extraction: isolate Tyler, no one else touches anything, move to open
air, no mirrors, no screens.

Tyler wants scissors and a sink.

Cam wants one clean shot of the glowing under Tyler's sleeve because if this turns into one of
those nights and they survive it, proof will matter.

The assistant wants to call 911 and keeps almost doing it, thumb hovering over the screen.

The sound guy keeps repeating, softer each time, "Don't name it. Don't frame it. Don't—"

Tyler grabs his sleeve and yanks it up to the elbow.

The forearm is clean skin except for a patch of sticky sheen the size of a playing card. It's
perfectly rectangular now, smooth as laminated film, adhered to him without edge or seam.

Inside it, moving like trapped video beneath ice, is a text thread.

Flip phone UI. Tiny, greenish monochrome. Two names at the top line are partially clipped by the
edge of the "screen," but the crew can read enough:

SUIT
HANA

No one speaks.

The messages begin to scroll on Tyler's arm.

Not all at once. Live.

SUIT: wrong order again
SUIT: black green red yellow white
SUIT: tell her before they peel it
HANA: i havent met him yet
SUIT: you did in the version that leaks
SUIT: this is the sticky one

Tyler looks from his arm to Hana.

His voice is almost gentle, which is how you know he's furious.

"'I haven't met him yet'?"

Hana goes pale in a way the camera can't fake.

"Everybody eyes down," she says.

No one moves.

The text continues.

HANA: what is it actually
SUIT: failed separation medium
SUIT: memory adhesive
SUIT: if he touches the edge it starts indexing

Tyler is already staring at the lower-right corner of the rectangular patch.

Because of course he is.

Because human beings are built to test warnings.

Because the patch has a corner and corners are invitations.

"Tyler," Hana says, and this time there is no calm left in her voice. "Do not touch the edge."

He doesn't look up.

"Did you know this was going to happen?"

The messages on his arm stutter, as if buffering.

Then one final line appears, wider than the patch should allow, forcing itself into tiny
compressed text:

SUIT: if he reads this he already touched it

Tyler's thumb twitches.

Not a decision. A reflex.

The pad of his thumb brushes the corner.

For one impossible instant, the patch lifts.

Not off his skin.

Off the scene.

Like someone peeled a layer of reality by a millimeter and every surface in the garage shifted
out of registration—pillar to the left, rail to the right, Cam duplicated in the lens and in the
stairwell, emergency light now red instead of green.

The assistant screams.

Cam catches one frame—one single frame—of a second Hana standing across the garage, older, blood
on her sleeve, already shouting the thing this Hana is about to say.

"DON'T LET HIM—"

The peeled edge snaps wider.

Everything goes sticky.

*CUT TO BLACK.*
