"attends the way the Council does everything now: in writing, once, briefly." Its real,
documented first appearance in that episode was a single page, delivered by a clerk, reading "The
Council submits one question and will not speak further," followed by one real question in full

the established precedent exactly, is in how the people IN the room answer a question the Council
itself will not elaborate on.

## ACT I — THE CARD HAS FINISHED FADING

Tyler holds the blank card up to the light one more time, checking, the way you check a lottery
ticket you already know has lost.

Nothing. Not even a ghost of the word. Whatever "UNDOCUMENTED" was, it's finished being visible.

**TYLER**
Is it gone, or did I just stop being allowed to see it?

Hana, beside him on the lobby bench, doesn't look over.

**HANA**
With this building, those are usually the same question.

**TYLER**
Is that comforting?

**HANA**
No. But at least it's consistent.

The lobby is doing its ordinary-institution impression again — brochures, a bulletin board for
events that may or may not have happened, a receptionist who has never once looked up at either
of them.

Tyler turns the dead card over in his fingers.

**TYLER**
Do you think the Owl's coming back?

**HANA**
She said ask again when the second case closes. That's not a woman who circles back early.

**TYLER**
Is that faction pride or just her personality?

**HANA**
With Owls, I've stopped trying to separate the two.

---

## ACT II — ONE PAGE

The receptionist's head lifts. First time. The whole lobby seems to notice the movement before
anyone consciously registers why.

A figure crosses the floor — plain clothes, no badge, no ring, carrying nothing but a single
sheet of paper held flat, the way you'd carry something that might spill.

**HANA**
(under her breath)
That's not Academy staff.

**TYLER**
Who is it?

**HANA**
A Clerk. I've heard the word. I've never seen the job.

The Clerk stops in front of them — not the Man, not the Archivist, not anyone either of them
recognizes — and extends the page toward Hana with both hands, the specific formality of someone
who has been told exactly how many centimeters of distance the ritual requires.

**CLERK**
For the custody matter. Both names.

Hana takes it like it might be warm, or cold, or nothing at all, and it turns out to be nothing
at all, which is somehow worse.

**HANA**
Is there a response required?

**CLERK**
There is never a response required. There is only a record of whether one was offered.

The Clerk turns and leaves before either of them can ask anything else — the door swinging shut
on an ordinary hinge, in an ordinary way, like nothing about the last ninety seconds happened.

Tyler leans in to read over Hana's shoulder.

**TYLER**
What does it say?

Hana's voice goes very quiet, very careful, the way people read things they already suspect will
be repeated back to them later.

**HANA**
"The Council submits one question and will not speak further."

**TYLER**
And the question?

Hana turns the page so he can see it himself.

**WHO WITNESSED THE WITNESS?**

---

## ACT III — NINETEEN SECONDS

Neither of them says anything for a while. Tyler, later, would not be able to say how long —
which turns out to be the point.

**TYLER**
Is that meant for me?

**HANA**
It's meant for whoever answers it first. That's usually how these work.

**TYLER**
Do you know what it means?

Hana reads it again, like the words might rearrange themselves into something kinder on a second
pass. They don't.

**HANA**
I know what it's asking around. Not what it wants.

**TYLER**
What's it asking around?

**HANA**
Green spreads when witnessed. That's your classification. The debt confirms its amount by being
witnessed. That's the Ledger. Everything in this building runs on someone watching something.

**TYLER**
So the question is asking who watched all of that happening.

**HANA**
Or it's asking whether anyone ever really did. Whether the watching was ever real, or just
filed as real because filing was easier than checking.

Tyler's mouth goes dry.

**TYLER**
Is that worse?

**HANA**
It's the same size of bad, dressed as a different question.

The Archivist's voice arrives before she does, from the stairwell doorway, unhurried as always.

**ARCHIVIST**
May I see it?

---

## ACT IV — THREE ANSWERS, NONE OF THEM FINAL

Hana hands over the page without hesitation — a real, small trust, the kind that wouldn't have
existed a week ago.

The Archivist reads it once. Something crosses her face that isn't quite recognition and isn't
quite dread — closer to the specific fatigue of someone hearing an old accusation phrased in new
handwriting.

**ARCHIVIST**
I know this question. Not this exact one. Its shape.

**TYLER**
What's the shape?

**ARCHIVIST**
It's the question you ask when you already suspect the watcher never earned the right to look.

**HANA**
Is that an answer or an accusation?

**ARCHIVIST**
With the Council, those have always been the same document.

The Owl's voice, when it comes, comes from nowhere anyone saw her enter — she's simply there now,
by the reception desk, coffee cup long since abandoned.

**OWL**
I'll answer it literally, since nobody else seems inclined to.

She takes the page, reads it, hands it back without ceremony.

**OWL**
The Eastwind Owls witnessed the witness. We have logged Emily OS's every architectural
intervention since before this Academy poured its own foundation. If the question is asking who
watched the Ledger confirm its own amount — it was us. It's always been us. We simply never filed
a report anyone here was cleared to read.

Tyler stares at her.

**TYLER**
Is that the real answer?

**OWL**
It's a true one. Whether it's the one the Council wants is a separate question, and not mine to
guess at.

The Man appears in the doorway to the circular room — smaller again, the recalibrated-number look
from the diner returned and settled in for good, this time.

**MAN**
That page was not meant to circulate.

**HANA**
It's a custody document with both our names on it. It circulates to us by definition.

**MAN**
The Council will not appreciate multiple parties answering on the record.

**ARCHIVIST**
The Council didn't ask for one answer. It asked one question. There's a difference, and you of
all people should have learned it by now.

---

## ACT V — WHO WATCHES THE WATCHERS OF THE WATCHERS

Tyler looks around the room — Hana, the Archivist, the Owl, the Man — and understands, all at
once, the actual shape of the question in a way none of the individual answers quite gave him.

**TYLER**
Can I try?

Every head turns.

**HANA**
It's not usually offered to the asset.

**TYLER**
I'm not asking for permission. I'm asking if it's worth anything if I do.

Nobody stops him. That itself feels like an answer.

**TYLER**
Nobody witnessed the witness. That's what none of you are saying directly. The Owls logged it.
The Ledger confirmed it. Emily OS ran it. But nobody with the authority to make it MATTER ever
actually looked at the looking. It just kept getting filed one level further down, forever,
because everyone assumed someone above them already checked.

The room is very quiet.

**TYLER**
So the honest answer is: nobody did. Not once. Not ever. It's witnesses all the way down and an
empty room at the bottom.

The Archivist's expression, for the second time this season, breaks its own stillness.

**ARCHIVIST**
That's not the answer I expected from the person the whole file is about.

**TYLER**
Is it the right one?

**ARCHIVIST**
It's the first one today that sounded like it cost you something to say.

---

## ACT VI — THE SECOND PAGE

The Clerk returns. Same door, same hinge, same careful two-handed carry — except this time, the
page is already open, already resolved, no ceremony left to spend.

**CLERK**
The Council received an answer.

**HANA**
Which one?

**CLERK**
It does not specify. It never does. It specifies only that one was received, and that the record
now reflects it.

The Clerk sets the second page on the reception desk and turns to leave.

**TYLER**
Wait. What does this one say?

The Clerk pauses at the door — the only hesitation Tyler has seen from anyone claiming to
represent the Council, in either appearance.

**CLERK**
It says the review of Academy protocol is suspended. Not closed. Suspended.

**MAN**
(quiet, to no one)
Suspended pending what.

**CLERK**
Pending a witness with the authority to matter.

The Clerk leaves. The door swings shut on its ordinary hinge one more time.

Tyler looks at Hana. Hana looks at the second page, unmoving on the desk, like she's afraid
touching it might undo whatever just happened.

**TYLER**
Is that good?

Hana's answer comes slowly, like she's testing whether the words will hold her weight before she
puts her full trust on them.

**HANA**
It means somebody's still deciding whether either of us counts as one.

Outside, through the lobby's tall windows, the afternoon light hasn't changed at all — the same
patient, ordinary gold it's been since the first morning, entirely unconcerned with how many
pages have crossed how many desks in between.

**TYLER**
Do we wait for that too?

Hana finally reaches out and takes the second page off the desk, folding it once, careful, like
something that might still be worth keeping even half-understood.

**HANA**
We don't wait for anything anymore. We just don't call it running.

*Cut to black.*
