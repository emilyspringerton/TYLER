**REAL, HONEST CONTINUITY NOTE — a deliberate, load-bearing crossover, not a coincidence:**
This episode ties NLE01's own crow-wing wall panel directly to an already-existing piece of this
universe's own mythology — hero #68 in `multiverse_heroes.md`, **"The Morrigan, Once Refused"**
(seed phrase: *"refused once, present ever since"*; Δφ crow-form readings cluster at 31°, Golden
Band; her own "Washer at the Ford" aspect, traditionally an omen washing the blood of those about
to die — except once, when she's found washing something CLEAN for someone who hasn't died and
isn't going to, "and won't explain why the ritual ran without its usual ending"). NEVER_LAND's own
established imagery — a crow behind a sealed wall, a debt that "collects... proof that we were
here," Hana's own closing line in NLE01 that Never_Land "is a refusal" — reads as a real match for
that same figure, not a new one invented from scratch. This episode treats that match as true
in-universe, not a fan-theory aside: what's collecting from Tyler is a real, already-canon force,
now brought explicitly into the Academy's own architecture.

## ACT I — THE NOTE, STILL THERE

Morning does not arrive so much as get announced. The dorm room's single window — the one that
faces a wall — brightens by exactly the amount a real window would, on a schedule nothing outside
seems to be running.

The charcoal note is still on the desk. **PAY WHAT YOU SAW.** The letters have not smudged.
Tyler checked, twice, in the night — dragging one knuckle across the words to see if this was
paint, chalk, a trick. It is not a trick. It is exactly as permanent as it needs to be and no
more.

Hana is asleep, or something close to it, sitting upright in the room's one chair, arms folded,
like she posted herself there sometime after the corridor and never clocked out.

Tyler watches her for a while before he says anything.

**TYLER**
Do Handlers sleep?

Hana's eyes open. She was not asleep.

**HANA**
Do you think I'd let myself?

**TYLER**
Around me?

**HANA**
Around anyone.

Tyler swings his legs off the bed. His feet find the floor like the floor might not hold.

**TYLER**
Did anything come in the night?

Hana's gaze goes to the note before he even finishes the question.

**HANA**
Besides that?

**TYLER**
Is "besides that" a yes?

Hana stands. Her joints don't crack, exactly, but there's a small sound like something settling
back into a shape it was holding all night.

**HANA**
There was a smell. Around four.

**TYLER**
What kind of smell?

**HANA**
Wet stone.

Tyler's stomach does something small and cold.

**TYLER**
Is that bad?

**HANA**
It's specific.

**TYLER**
Specific like a name?

Hana doesn't answer that. She crosses to the door, checks the corridor with a glance that's
mostly habit, and holds it open.

**HANA**
Get up. We're not staying for whatever comes to collect a note like that in person.

## ACT II — THE ROOM NOBODY TOLD THEM ABOUT

The corridor outside is the same and isn't. Tyler counts doors the way he counts exits — REGISTRY,
SEALS, NAMES, all present, all where they were — until he reaches one that wasn't there yesterday,
or was there and unlabeled, which somehow feels worse.

The new door has no sign. Just a damp line along the bottom, like water's been run under it
recently and not entirely mopped up.

Tyler stops.

**TYLER**
Was this here before?

**HANA**
Everything is here before. That's the whole design.

**TYLER**
Is that a yes?

Hana doesn't move toward the door. If anything, she puts herself very slightly between it and
Tyler, which is its own kind of answer.

**HANA**
Don't.

**TYLER**
You say that about every door.

**HANA**
I say it about the ones that are actually doors.

Tyler tilts his head.

**TYLER**
And the ones that aren't?

**HANA**
Those don't need a warning. They just don't open.

Tyler reaches past her before she can stop him — not fast, not sly, just simple, the way he does
everything that's going to hurt someone. He touches the handle.

It's warm. Warmer than the Archivist door was. Warm like something recently held.

The door opens on its own, an inch, releasing a smell Tyler now understands without needing Hana
to name it. Wet stone. And under that, faint: iron. Not blood exactly. The idea of blood, filed
and put away.

Inside: a room shaped like a laundry room, if a laundry room had been designed by someone who'd
only ever heard one described. A single basin. A single line strung wall to wall. On the line:
one white cloth, dripping, clean.

Standing at the basin, back to the door, is a figure Tyler has not seen before.

She doesn't turn around.

**ARCHIVIST**
You're early.

Hana's whole body goes rigid.

**HANA**
He's not supposed to be here at all.

**ARCHIVIST**
And yet.

The Archivist turns. She is unremarkable in exactly the way the Man is unremarkable — a face
built for forgettability — except for her eyes, which have the specific stillness of someone who
has watched the same scene enough times to stop being surprised by any of its endings.

**ARCHIVIST**
Tyler.

**TYLER**
Everyone here knows my name before I say it.

**ARCHIVIST**
That's not knowing. That's remembering.

**TYLER**
Is there a difference?

**ARCHIVIST**
Knowing happens once. Remembering happens again and again until you stop flinching.

Tyler's eyes go to the cloth on the line.

**TYLER**
What is that?

**ARCHIVIST**
Something I wash for people who haven't earned the usual reason.

**TYLER**
What's the usual reason?

The Archivist looks at him for a long moment — long enough that Hana steps half a pace forward,
like she might physically get between the look and Tyler if she has to.

**ARCHIVIST**
Dying.

Tyler doesn't flinch. He's had longer than most to practice not flinching.

**TYLER**
And I haven't.

**ARCHIVIST**
Not yet.

**TYLER**
So why wash anything?

The Archivist's mouth does something that isn't a smile but remembers being one, once, a long
time ago, for someone else.

**ARCHIVIST**
Because the ritual doesn't care whether you've earned it. It only cares whether it's owed.

## ACT III — WHAT THE CROW IS

Hana finally speaks, and her voice has lost the flatness she's worn since the steps outside the
school.

**HANA**
You're not supposed to be operating inside Academy walls.

**ARCHIVIST**
I'm not operating. I'm washing.

**HANA**
That's not a distinction anyone else recognizes.

**ARCHIVIST**
It's the only one that's ever mattered.

Tyler looks between them.

**TYLER**
Do you two know each other?

**HANA**
Everyone who works custody knows what she is.

**TYLER**
What is she?

Hana's jaw works like the word costs something to spend.

**HANA**
The one who shows up when someone's about to lose a fight they don't know they're in.

The Archivist doesn't deny it. She wrings the cloth once, gently, though it doesn't drip any
differently for the wringing.

**ARCHIVIST**
I offered myself to a man once. Help, and more than help. He said no. He didn't know who was
asking.

**TYLER**
What happened?

**ARCHIVIST**
I stayed anyway. In shapes he didn't recognize. Underfoot. Overhead. Once as an old woman he
healed without knowing what he was closing.

**TYLER**
Did he ever find out?

**ARCHIVIST**
At the very end. When it was too late for it to change anything, which is when everyone finds
out everything that matters.

Tyler's eyes go to the crow's-wing panel image still burned into his mind from the corridor the
day before.

**TYLER**
Is that what the feathers were? You?

**ARCHIVIST**
I don't confirm sightings. I confirm endings.

**TYLER**
Is this an ending?

The Archivist looks at the cloth on the line — clean, dripping, waiting for nothing in particular
— and for the first time her stillness cracks, just slightly, into something that might be
curiosity.

**ARCHIVIST**
I don't know yet. That's the part I don't have a name for either.

## ACT IV — THE DEADLINE

The Man's voice arrives before he does, down the corridor, sharp in a way it wasn't yesterday.

**MAN (O.S.)**
Handler Hana.

Hana's whole posture changes — not fear exactly, but the specific readiness of someone who has
rehearsed this exact hallway many times in her head.

**HANA**
(low, to Tyler)
Don't say where we were.

**TYLER**
Why?

**HANA**
Because she's not supposed to exist to him. Only to us.

The Man arrives in the doorway. He takes in the basin, the cloth, the Archivist, and Tyler,
standing far too close to all three, and his professional calm slips by exactly one degree.

**MAN**
This wing is sealed.

**ARCHIVIST**
Wings don't stay sealed. They molt.

The Man ignores her entirely — a real, pointed choice, the kind of not-looking that's its own
answer.

**MAN**
Hana. Your assessment window closes at the next tone.

Hana's throat moves.

**HANA**
I need more time.

**MAN**
The Academy doesn't extend custody windows for sentiment.

**TYLER**
Is that what this is? Sentiment?

The Man turns to Tyler with the patient, terrible smile of someone who has never once lost an
argument by raising his voice.

**MAN**
Ask her.

Tyler looks at Hana. Hana looks at the floor, then at the cloth on the line, then, finally, at
Tyler.

**HANA**
It's not sentiment.

**MAN**
Then what is it?

Hana's answer is so quiet the room has to lean in to catch it.

**HANA**
It's the only thing I've ever wanted that wasn't assigned to me.

The Man's smile doesn't move, but something behind it recalculates.

**MAN**
Then you understand exactly what the debt is going to ask for.

## ACT V — WATCHED ANYWAY

They leave the Man in the wing with the Archivist — an image Tyler can't stop turning over, the
two of them left alone together, one who files endings and one who assigns them, like watching
two different definitions of the same job shake hands.

In the corridor, Tyler's phone — which shouldn't have signal, hasn't had signal since they walked
in — buzzes once.

He looks. A notification. No app name. No sender.

**"THE WASHING HAS BEGUN. WITNESSES: 1."**

Tyler shows Hana the screen. Her face doesn't change, which is worse than if it had.

**HANA**
That's you. You're the witness.

**TYLER**
Is that bad?

**HANA**
Witnessing is how the debt confirms the amount owed.

**TYLER**
So by looking, I made it real?

**HANA**
You made it billable.

Tyler laughs, once, without much humor in it.

**TYLER**
Is there a version of this where I just don't look at anything, ever again?

Hana's eyes flick to his, and for a second there's something almost tender under the exhaustion.

**HANA**
There's a version where you stop being the kind of person things happen in front of.

**TYLER**
Is that a real option?

**HANA**
No.

---

## ACT VI — THE FIRST RUN

They don't discuss it. They just start walking faster.

Hana leads them not toward the Registry, not toward the lobby they came in through, but toward a
stairwell Tyler hasn't seen — narrow, unlit except by a strip along the floor, the kind of
architecture that exists specifically for leaving.

**TYLER**
Is this the run?

**HANA**
This is the beginning of finding out if there's a run.

They take the stairs two at a time. Behind them, distantly, a sound: water, running somewhere it
shouldn't be, a basin filling that they left empty.

At the top of the stairwell: a door. Ordinary. Metal. A push bar, the kind that means *fire exit,
alarm will sound* even though no sign says so here.

Tyler puts his hand on it.

**TYLER**
Do you know what's on the other side?

Hana's hand covers his on the bar. Her voice is steady in the specific way that only holds
because she's decided it has to.

**HANA**
Daylight. Probably. Maybe the same steps we came up.

**TYLER**
And if it's not?

**HANA**
Then we find out what the debt does to people who don't wait for it to ask nicely.

Tyler looks at her — really looks, one more time, the way he did in the stairwell the night
before, memorizing a version of her he might not get to keep.

**TYLER**
Whatever's out there. Are you coming?

Hana's answer arrives before her voice does, in the way her fingers tighten over his on the bar.

**HANA**
I told you. It's the only thing I've ever wanted that wasn't assigned to me.

They push the door together.

White light floods the stairwell — too bright, too total, the kind of light that isn't weather.

*Cut to black.*
