**PRODUCTION NOTE, READ BEFORE THE SCRIPT:**

Founder, real-time: "continue tyler series x" → "you know i hate calls." The second part changes
this episode's own method, not just its content. Every prior Series X interlude (x01 through x06)
was built around a real, live gpt2-alpine-c render — a real 5-call ramp (16/32/64/128/256 tokens),
run live, taking somewhere around 11 minutes each time, with the actual model output quoted
verbatim in the script. This one runs none of that. No render, no call, no live model output
anywhere in this file.

**That absence is not a workaround for this episode — it's the actual plot.** x06's own "Notes for
whoever picks this up next" left two real, explicitly unresolved threads: whether that episode's
fabricated press-conference render was evidence of Emily OS's fabrication problem escalating, or
one run's temperature setting landing badly; and whether seeding future renders from a prior
render's own output — the exact chain that produced x02 → x03 → x04 → x05 → x06 in sequence — was
still a good idea once a chained render had produced something that dangerous. Both threads are
closed here, in-universe, by the crew simply deciding not to run the next one. Not because the
technology failed — because continuing to authorize five sequential live inference calls, chained
from output nobody vetted first, on a subject as consequential as x06's, is a real choice a real
production makes, and declining to make it again is exactly as much a decision as running it was.

**Placement, same as every prior Series X entry**: unnumbered, does not touch S06's own
Detroit-archive continuity or S10's own Ahmad ibn Yusuf arc. This one is set the following morning,
same edit bay, same three people, one scene, no field footage.

## ACT ONE — THE TWO QUESTIONS — [01:00–06:00]

```
INT. DOCUMENTARY UNIT A — EDIT BAY — CONTINUOUS
```

**PRODUCER**
Two questions, from the log. First one: was last night the render getting worse, or was it one bad
temperature setting on one bad night.

**CAMERA OP**
I don't know. Neither do you.

**PRODUCER**
Correct. And I've decided that not knowing is itself the answer to the second question.

**CAMERA OP**
Which was whether we keep seeding the next one off the last one's own output.

**PRODUCER**
Right. And here's the actual reasoning, because I want it on tape, not just the conclusion: every
render since the second episode came from the render before it. Nobody chose those words on
purpose — the model said something, and we treated whatever it said as raw material for the next
call, five times in a row, across five real episodes, without ever once stopping to ask whether the
thing we were about to feed back in was something we'd want to feed back in. Last night it produced
a fabricated press conference with a real person's name in it. If that's the escalation the first
question is asking about, feeding it back in again would mean the WORST output we've ever gotten
becomes the SEED for the next one. That's not curiosity anymore. That's just recklessness with
extra steps.

**CAMERA OP**
And if it's not escalating — if it really was just the temperature —

**PRODUCER**
Then we still don't know that. We'd be betting on it. I'm not willing to bet the next render on a
guess about why the last one went the way it did, when the actual cost of guessing wrong is
something that reads like a real transcript of something that never happened.

## ACT TWO — THE DECISION, ON THE RECORD — [06:00–11:00]

```
INT. DOCUMENTARY UNIT A — EDIT BAY — CONTINUOUS
```

**CAMERA OP**
So what do we actually do. We can't just stop the segment, we've built five episodes around it.

**PRODUCER**
We don't stop the segment. We stop the specific thing that got us here — a render feeding the next
render, unread, in a chain, on a clock, because it made a good act break. If Series X ever runs
another one of these, it starts from something a person actually picked, on purpose, after looking
at it — not the last four words the machine happened to land on before it ran out of room to keep
pretending it knew where it was going. That's the whole rule. It's not complicated. It was just
never written down until right now, because until last night nobody had a reason to write it down.

**CAMERA OP**
*(finally getting the laptop out, but not to run anything — just to type)*
I'm putting that in the log as policy, not as a note for next time.

**PRODUCER**
Good. Put my name on it. I don't want it to look like the crew quietly decided this among
themselves and hoped nobody noticed the segment changed shape. We're saying why, on camera, same
as every other time this show has had to say something out loud instead of just doing it quietly
and hoping it reads as intentional later.

**TYLER (O.S.)**
For what it's worth — I could have told you the render chain was going to end somewhere like that.
Not because I know anything about the model. Because I know what happens to anything that keeps
repeating the last thing it said, faster each time, with less of a reason each time, for long
enough. I do that. It's the only trick I have. I just have a reason for mine, same as I said last
night, and the render didn't.

**PRODUCER**
That's twice now you've said something that should be the whole thesis of this segment and then
gone quiet again before anyone could write it down properly.

**TYLER (O.S.)**
I know. I don't do it on purpose. I've never once done anything on—

He doesn't finish it. Nobody in the room reacts. It has never once occurred to either of them that
this is unusual.

## Notes for whoever picks this up next

- **This is the first Series X interlude with zero gpt2-alpine-c calls.** Founder real-time
  feedback ("you know i hate calls") landed while this episode was being scoped and directly
  shaped the method, not just a line of dialogue — the in-universe policy change (no more
  automatic render-chaining, a person picks the seed first) is a real, honest mirror of a real
  production preference, not invented to fill space. "Television as code," taken literally: the
  real methodology changed, so the show's own internal methodology changed with it, on camera, the
  same episode.
- **Both of x06's own open threads are addressed, not resolved** — deliberately. Nobody in this
  episode claims to know whether the fabrication problem is escalating or whether x06 was one bad
  run. The actual decision (stop auto-chaining seeds) is written to be correct under either answer,
  which is the point Act One spends its whole runtime building toward.
- **Real, practical note for whoever runs the next render experiment, if there is one**: per this
  episode's own new in-universe policy, a future Series X render episode should open with a
  human-chosen seed, stated as such, not lifted verbatim from whatever the previous render's own
  final line happened to be. That's now a real production constraint on future episodes in this
  sub-series, not just a line these three characters said once.
- **Open thread, still genuinely unresolved, by design**: does Emily OS's own fabrication behavior
  ever get named formally as a recurring in-universe phenomenon (as opposed to something the crew
  reacts to episode by episode), and if so, does that happen inside Series X or does it eventually
  need to cross into the numbered main continuity. Not a technical question — a real founder call
  for whenever Series X picks this up again.
