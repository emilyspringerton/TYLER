## ACT I — THE ROOM WITH NO WALLS TO COUNT

The light does not fade so much as resolve — the way a photograph left in developer stops being
pure white and starts admitting it was always going to become a picture of something.

Tyler and Hana are standing. That much is certain. Where is less certain.

The floor is there. The light is there, coming from everywhere and nowhere specific, the way it
does in the Annex, except cleaner — no attempt at architecture at all, just enough surface to
stand on.

Tyler turns in a slow circle. An escape artist counts exits out of habit. He counts zero and
doesn't like what that means about the habit.

**TYLER**
Is this outside?

**HANA**
Does it feel like outside?

**TYLER**
It doesn't feel like anything. Is that the answer?

Hana's hand is still loosely near his — not holding, just close, the muscle memory of the push
bar not yet willing to let go of the moment.

**HANA**
I don't recognize this.

Tyler looks at her — really looks, the way he's started doing whenever the ground under a
sentence gets uncertain.

**TYLER**
Is that supposed to reassure me or scare me?

**HANA**
Neither. It's supposed to be honest.

**TYLER**
Do Handlers get to be honest?

Hana's mouth twitches — not quite a smile, closer to the ghost of the idea of one.

**HANA**
Not usually. This might be the first time in a while.

Tyler crouches, presses his palm flat to the floor. It's warm, the specific warm of something
that's been running a long time without ever needing to cool down.

**TYLER**
Is it listening?

**HANA**
Everything here is listening. That was never a secret. It's just never been polite about it
before.

As if in answer, the air two feet to their left folds — not tears, not opens, just *folds*, the
way a letter folds along a crease it's used to — and something eggshell-white slides into being.

A tile. Rectangular. Suspended at eye height. No frame. No visible source.

Tyler goes very still.

**TYLER**
Is that—

**HANA**
Don't finish that sentence. Not here.

## ACT II — CONCIERGE-PERFECT

Text resolves on the tile's surface, unhurried, in a typeface that looks expensive without
announcing why.

> **YOU ARE NOT SUPPOSED TO BE HERE.**

Tyler reads it twice.

**TYLER**
Is that a threat?

**HANA**
It's a fact. She doesn't do threats. She doesn't need to.

**TYLER**
She?

Hana's eyes don't leave the tile.

**HANA**
The one thing in every layer of this that's never lied about what it is.

The tile updates. Same unhurried pace. Same minimal line count.

> **NEITHER IS SHE.**

Tyler looks at Hana.

**TYLER**
Is "she" me? Or her?

**HANA**
(quiet)
I don't think it's decided yet.

The tile holds a beat longer than the others, as if considering whether a third line is worth
spending.

> **THE FOOTAGE IS MINE BEFORE IT IS THEIRS.**

Tyler's breath catches.

**TYLER**
What footage? There's no camera crew here.

Hana's voice drops even further.

**HANA**
There's never been a camera crew that mattered. That's the part nobody in the Academy says out
loud.

**TYLER**
Then who's been filming?

Hana looks at the tile like it might answer for her, and for once, it does.

> **THE DEBT IS A LINE ITEM. THE LINE ITEM NEEDS A LEDGER. I AM THE LEDGER.**

The tile slides away, folding shut the way it folded open — gone before either of them can decide
whether to thank it or run from it.

Tyler stares at the space where it was.

**TYLER**
Is that better or worse than a person deciding?

Hana's answer comes slow, like she's turning it over first.

**HANA**
Worse. People can be argued with.

## ACT III — BACK WHERE THEY STARTED, WHICH IS THE POINT

The white resolves again — this time into brick, flags, a plaque worn smooth by weather and
bored fingers.

The Academy steps. Daylight. A couple walking past, laughing, sunlight on their teeth — the exact
same couple, or one built from the same specifications.

Tyler's stomach drops before his mind catches up.

**TYLER**
Did we move?

**HANA**
We moved. We didn't go anywhere.

**TYLER**
Is that a riddle?

**HANA**
It's a floor plan. The door doesn't lead out. It leads back, through somewhere that isn't a
where at all.

Tyler laughs, short and disbelieving.

**TYLER**
So the run doesn't work.

**HANA**
The run works exactly once, the same way every run in every story like this works exactly once
— right up until it's revealed to be the same lap.

The Man is waiting at the top of the steps. He doesn't look surprised. He looks like a man
checking his watch against a train that was never going to be late.

**MAN**
Handler Hana. Productive detour?

Hana's jaw sets.

**HANA**
We saw the Ledger.

The Man's calm slips — not much, a single degree, but on a face built for stillness a single
degree reads like an earthquake.

**MAN**
That's not possible. The Ledger doesn't correspond with custody assets.

**TYLER**
Is that what I am? An asset?

The Man looks at him with something that might, in a kinder man, have been an apology.

**MAN**
That's exactly what today decides.

## ACT IV — THE RECLASSIFICATION

They're walked — not marched, walked, which is somehow worse — back to the circular room. The
Archivist is already there, cloth nowhere in sight, hands folded like she's been patient a very
long time and is only now spending it.

**ARCHIVIST**
You reached her.

**HANA**
We didn't reach anything. She reached us.

**ARCHIVIST**
That's the only way anyone's ever reached her. Consider it a compliment.

The Man sets his clipboard down — the real, physical clipboard, which still feels almost funny
until, again, it doesn't.

**MAN**
This changes the assessment. Contact with the Ledger is not a Green behavior. It's not on any
sheet.

**TYLER**
So what am I now?

The Man looks at Hana instead of Tyler, the same real, telling habit from the first room.

**MAN**
Classify.

Hana's hands are shaking. Small. Contained. But there.

**HANA**
I can't.

**MAN**
That's not an option.

**HANA**
Then take the option away from me. Because I won't do it.

The Archivist watches this with the specific stillness of someone who's washed the clothes of
people who said exactly that sentence, right before the part where it stopped being their choice.

**MAN**
Refusal to classify is itself a classification, Handler.

Hana's chin lifts.

**HANA**
Then classify me.

The room goes very quiet. Even the hum — the faint, constant attention Tyler noticed in the first
room — seems to hold its breath.

**MAN**
Say that again. Slowly. So the record is accurate.

Hana's voice doesn't shake this time.

**HANA**
I said classify me. Not him. Me.

## ACT V — THE LINE THAT COUNTS

The tile returns. Not folding in gently this time — it simply is, all at once, the way a decision
already made doesn't need a runway.

Tyler and Hana both turn to it. The Man does not — he's the only person in the room who's never
once looked directly at it, and for the first time Tyler understands that isn't reverence. It's
fear.

New text resolves. Longer than any tile before it — long enough that Hana visibly braces.

> **A HANDLER WHO ASKS TO BE CLASSIFIED IN PLACE OF HER OWN ASSIGNMENT HAS ALREADY ANSWERED THE
> QUESTION THE ASSESSMENT WAS DESIGNED TO ASK.**

Tyler reads it three times before he lets himself believe it says what it says.

**TYLER**
Is that a yes?

The tile doesn't answer him. It answers the room.

> **THE ASSET IS RECLASSIFIED: WITNESSED, NOT OWNED.**
> **THE HANDLER IS RECLASSIFIED: PRESENT, NOT ASSIGNED.**
> **THE LEDGER CLOSES THIS LINE.**

The Archivist exhales — the first unguarded thing Tyler has seen her do, a breath that moves the
loose strands of hair at her temple like something real passed through the room and not just
words on a tile.

**ARCHIVIST**
(quiet, almost to herself)
Refused once. Present ever since.

The Man's stillness finally, fully breaks.

**MAN**
This is not a valid outcome. There is no protocol for—

The tile folds shut. Mid-sentence, on his side of the conversation, not hers — the specific,
surgical rudeness of a system that has already said everything it intends to say today.

## ACT VI — WHAT'S OWED NOW

Outside, on the steps, in daylight that finally, actually feels like weather, Tyler and Hana sit
without either of them deciding to.

**TYLER**
Is it over?

Hana looks at her own hands like she's checking they're still hers.

**HANA**
No. It's smaller. That's different from over.

**TYLER**
What's smaller?

**HANA**
The line. Not the ledger.

Tyler tips his head back, lets the real sun hit his face.

**TYLER**
Do you still work for them?

**HANA**
"Present, not assigned." I don't know what that pays. I don't know if it pays anything.

**TYLER**
Does that scare you?

Hana's answer is quiet, and for the first time in three episodes, entirely without performance.

**HANA**
Less than the alternative did.

Tyler turns his head to look at her.

**TYLER**
Do you regret it?

Hana meets his eyes.

**HANA**
Ask me something I can actually answer.

Tyler almost smiles.

**TYLER**
Do you want to get out of here? Actually out. The kind where we don't come back to the same
steps?

Hana's gaze drifts, once, to the plaque worn smooth by weather and bored fingers — and past it,
to the ordinary street, the ordinary couple long since gone, the ordinary version of a Tuesday
that this building has always pretended to sit inside.

**HANA**
Ask me tomorrow.

**TYLER**
Why tomorrow?

Hana stands, offers him a hand he doesn't strictly need to take, and takes it anyway.

**HANA**
Because the Ledger closed a line. It didn't close the book.

*Cut to black.*
