## ACT 1 — THE SECOND OF THE TWO ROOMS

The peel does not finish snapping.

It holds — one impossible inch of daylight between the garage that exists and the garage that is
trying to, both of them full of the same people standing in slightly wrong places, like a
photograph taken a half-second after someone flinched.

Tyler's thumb is still on the corner. He can't seem to lift it, not because anything is holding
him there, but because the decision to remove a finger from something is, it turns out, a whole
separate decision from the one that put it there.

The older Hana is still mid-word in the sliver of the other room. Her mouth is shaped like the
rest of "DON'T LET HIM—" but no more of the sentence arrives. She is looking at someone who isn't
Tyler. She is looking at someone standing exactly where Tyler is standing.

"Tyler." Hana's voice, the real one, the one actually in the room with him. "Let go of the
corner."

"I'm trying."

"Try faster."

Cam has not stopped filming, because Cam has never once in his life figured out how to stop
filming, and this is either his greatest professional flaw or the only reason any of this will
ever be believed later.

The sound guy has both hands over his ears, which does nothing, because what's coming through
isn't sound. It's the specific, wrong pressure of a room that has briefly become two rooms
occupying one address.

## ACT 2 — WHAT THE PATCH SAYS NOW

The text on Tyler's forearm hasn't stopped. It's just gotten harder to read, because half the
letters are being drawn for a garage that hasn't happened yet and half for the one they're
standing in.

SUIT: he's not supposed to be able to hold it
SUIT: black green red yellow white
SUIT: wrong order again
SUIT: somebody started at yellow

Hana reads it upside down over his arm and her face does the thing it did in Act 2 of tonight
already — the thing where she stops pretending she doesn't know what she's looking at.

"Yellow," she says, mostly to herself. "Yellow is the part everyone forgets to plan for."

Tyler's eyes cut to her. "You know the whole list."

"I know four-fifths of a list I've spent a very long time not thinking about."

"And the fifth?"

"White. Nobody tells you the fifth until you've survived the first four. That's not a rule
someone wrote down. That's just how badly it goes if you skip ahead."

The patch flickers. For one frame, the greenish flip-phone text is replaced by something cleaner,
almost like a modal — a thin rectangle, calm sans-serif, nothing like the Suit's ragged typing:

IGNITION ORDER REQUIRED

Then it's gone, and the Suit's ragged texting comes back like static reasserting itself over a
clearer signal that isn't supposed to be reachable from here at all.

"Was that—" Cam starts.

"Don't," Hana says. "Don't name what you think you just saw. Not out loud. Not tonight."

The production assistant, to their enormous credit, has stopped reaching for their phone and
started doing the only useful thing available to a person with no training and no jurisdiction:
standing very still and not making it worse.

## ACT 3 — WHY HIM

The peeled inch widens, just slightly, just long enough for the older Hana's eyes to finally land
on Tyler instead of on whoever she was shouting at.

She doesn't finish the sentence. She looks, instead, like she's recalculating whether it needs
finishing at all.

Then the inch closes on her side, smooth as a held door letting go, and she's gone — not
collapsed, not erased, just no longer the room they're standing in anymore.

The garage is one garage again. The pillar's writing has gone still. The rail is just a rail.

Tyler's thumb comes off the corner at last, and the patch — the whole rectangle of it — goes
matte, inert, the color of a screen that's been switched off rather than a screen that was never
on.

He breathes like he forgot he was supposed to be doing it continuously.

"Why me," he says. Not dramatic. Genuinely asking, the way you ask a mechanic why your car and
not the one next to it.

Hana doesn't answer right away. When she does, she picks the words like she's defusing something.

"Because whatever that residue actually is, it needs a door that opens from both sides. Most
people are a wall. You're not."

"That's not an answer, that's a compliment shaped like a diagnosis."

"It's the only kind I have tonight."

She doesn't say *the seam.* She doesn't say *the only one who goes in and comes back.* Tyler
watches her not say it, and understands, with the specific clarity of someone who has learned to
read the shape of a withheld sentence, that she has a longer answer and has decided this is not
the parking structure where he gets it.

---

## ACT 4 — CLOSING TAG

Cam finally lowers the camera. His hands are shaking in a way that will look, on playback, exactly
like a technical problem with the rig, and everyone in the room will privately know it wasn't.

The sound guy checks his levels out of pure muscle memory and finds, at 12:04:47, four seconds of
audio that isn't any of their voices — a woman's voice, older, cut off mid-word, perfectly clean,
recorded by a boom mic that was, at that timestamp, pointed at the floor.

He doesn't play it for the others. Not yet. He just looks at the waveform for a long moment,
the way you look at a test result you already suspect the answer to.

The assistant finally speaks, quiet, mostly to fill the silence rather than to actually ask
anything.

"Are we— is this a wrap?"

Hana looks at the pillar. At Tyler's arm. At the ordinary, matte-dead rectangle of skin where a
window used to be.

"For tonight," she says. "The order's still wrong somewhere. That doesn't fix itself because we
stopped filming."

Tyler pulls his sleeve back down over the patch, the way you cover a wound you're not ready to
have opinions about yet.

"Somebody started at yellow," he says, half to himself, testing the shape of it. "What does that
even mean?"

Hana's answer is the last line of the night, and she says it already walking toward the exit, not
looking back to see if he follows.

"It means somebody skipped a step nobody's supposed to survive skipping. And it means, whatever
that costs, we're not the ones who get to decide when the bill comes due."

*Cut to black.*
