**PRODUCTION NOTE, READ BEFORE THE SCRIPT:**

Tuesday has no row in the Field's condensed table (see VH25). The Doc UI prints `NO ENTRY`, the
instruments read nothing, the Gap Meter shows a dash, and — this is the point — **Tyler's hum is not
audible to any instrument in the room.** On Tuesdays he is not being measured. He is just a man at
a table, and he knows it, and it is the only day he can hear himself think.

**Form: one take.** The cut counter in the Doc UI reads `CUTS: 0` from first frame to last. The
camera is handed from operator to operator (Cam, the PA, the sound guy, Hana) without a seam; the
script describes it as a single continuous move. Seventeen minutes forty-two seconds on the
timecode. It is the longest unbroken shot in the show.

**Content.** Tyler tells a story. It is about Toledo, 1040–1051 CE, and an Icelandic horse that
would not cross a bridge (`EPISODES.md`, Love Triangle #2: "Tyler / the Icelandic horse / Dawud
al-Muthanna's scholarly gaze"; VH10). Law 1 holds: it is a story about a horse. **Writer's Room
Rule 7's "one true thing" is spoken at the end and misunderstood by the character who hears it.**

**The Duck** has a Tuesday of its own. In VH11 it said it was always Tuesday in its town. Today the
statement is true, and for the first time in the show someone believes something the Duck says about
itself.

**The Tuesday line** ("If you keep filming, you become part of it," `README.md` epigraph) is held for
VH34. This episode is where it is *earned*.

**Not resolved:** the tenth ingredient; the sheet in the tray; the Gap Meter's meaning (it shows
nothing today, which means nothing).

*Cam has the rig up. It has been up for four seconds and it has already moved. The shot begins on
the sound guy's face, a foot from the lens, tapping a meter and frowning at it as one frowns at a
plate.*

**SOUND GUY**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Nothing.")*
Rien.

*The camera slides to the meter. The needle does not move. It is not at zero. It is not anywhere. A
tap, a second tap.*

**SOUND GUY (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Not seven eighty-three. Not twenty-eight seven. I can't even pick up the
room.")*
Pas de sept quatre-vingt-trois. Pas de vingt-huit sept. Je ne capte même pas la pièce.

**HANA (O.S.)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "It's Tuesday.")*
C'est mardi.

*The camera pans. The table: the upturned pot with a cloth over it, lunch laid on the cloth. Hana at
the head. The PA with the clipboard. The Duck on the sixth chair, feet not touching the floor,
a soup spoon held like a conductor's baton. The Producer's green thermos on the fourth chair, steam
standing from its mouth. And on the seventh, Tyler, with a heel of bread in one hand and the other
hand lying open on the cloth, doing nothing.*

**DUCK**
*(English)*
It's always a Tuesday in this town.

*Nobody remarks on this. It is simply true.*

**CAM (O.S., behind the lens)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "You've gone quiet.")*
Tu es devenu silencieux.

*The camera comes round to Tyler. His eyes are on the bread. He is not humming.*

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "I'm not.")*
Je ne le suis pas.

**SOUND GUY (O.S.)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "The hum.")*
Le fredonnement.

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED, without any weight: "On Tuesdays there's no row in the table. Nobody's measuring.
Not even me.")*
Le mardi, il n'y a pas de ligne dans le tableau. Personne ne mesure. Pas même moi.

**CAM (O.S.)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "How is that?")*
Comment ça se passe ?

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "It's the only day I can hear myself think.")*
C'est le seul jour où je m'entends penser.

*He looks up. The lens is on him, and he lets it. In the corner:*

*Hana puts her bread down and speaks without turning.*

**HANA**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Tell us about Toledo.")*
Raconte-nous Tolède.

*The camera, held out of sight at the PA's elbow, catches the half-second where he almost refuses.
It is the half-second where a man who has told the same story to many rooms decides that this is a
different room.*

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "It's not a long story. It's an eleven-year one.")*
Ce n'est pas une longue histoire. C'est une histoire de onze ans.

*He tears the bread in half.*

**TYLER (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Ten-forty. I came up from Portugal on a small horse. She'd been through three
owners by then. A trader brought her in from the north, from an island with no trees, and she'd
been sold twice since for being stubborn.")*
Mil quarante. Je suis remonté du Portugal sur un petit cheval. Elle avait eu trois propriétaires à
ce moment-là. Un marchand l'avait amenée du nord, d'une île sans arbres, et on l'avait revendue
deux fois parce qu'elle était têtue.

*Cam hands the rig to the PA with one movement, shoulder to shoulder, the lens never leaving its
subject. The picture lurches half an inch and is steady.*

**LOWER THIRD (DOC UI):**
```
OPERATOR: PA
```

**TYLER (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "There's a river outside Toledo. A bridge across it. A good one, Roman, an old
one. I walked her to it. She stopped at the first stone.")*
Il y a un fleuve devant Tolède. Un pont dessus. Un bon pont, romain, vieux. Je l'ai menée jusqu'à
lui. Elle s'est arrêtée à la première pierre.

*Behind him, unseen by him, the horse lifts her head. The camera finds her ear: swiveled, back,
listening to a thing about herself.*

**TYLER (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "I pulled. I begged. I tried apples. A week. She wouldn't cross.")*
J'ai tiré. J'ai supplié. J'ai essayé les pommes. Une semaine. Elle n'a pas traversé.

**PA (behind the lens)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "What did you do?")*
Qu'est-ce que tu as fait ?

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "I stayed. There was a village on the bank, they gave me a roof. I copied
things for a man who wanted them copied.")*
Je suis resté. Il y avait un village sur la rive, on m'a donné un toit. Je recopiais des choses pour
un homme qui voulait qu'on les recopie.

*The PA passes the rig to the sound guy without breaking the shot: a pivot, a hand, a hand.*

**TYLER (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Eleven years.")*
Onze ans.

*He lets that sit. In the old logic of the show, a man who says a number like that is about to
make it mean something. He doesn't. He eats a piece of bread.*

**TYLER (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Every morning I'd walk her down to the first stone. Every morning, no. And
every morning, she was still there the next day.")*
Chaque matin je la menais à la première pierre. Chaque matin, non. Et chaque matin, le lendemain,
elle était encore là.

*The sound guy, behind the lens, as he follows Tyler's hand from bread to table, lets the camera
tip an inch toward the horse. She is looking at the back of the man's head with an expression that
only a horse could not be accused of.*

**TYLER (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "In the eleventh winter she crossed. A Tuesday. Walked onto the bridge as if
she'd always meant to and I walked after her.")*
Au onzième hiver, elle a traversé. Un mardi. Elle est montée sur le pont comme si elle l'avait
toujours voulu, et je l'ai suivie.

C'était la chose la plus gentille qu'on m'ait jamais faite.

**HANA**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "A horse refusing you.")*
Un cheval qui te refuse.

*(French; SUBTITLED: "Everyone says yes. And means later.")*
Tout le monde dit oui. Et pense plus tard.

*He folds his hands on the cloth. They are not dry-washed. They are not folded to anyone.*

**TYLER (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "She said no and meant now. And then she was still there the next morning.
A no that's still there the next morning — you can lean on it.")*
Elle a dit non et elle le pensait maintenant. Et puis elle était encore là le lendemain matin. Un

*The camera, in Hana's hands, does not move.*

*It takes the table a beat to misunderstand it. The PA, from the side, gently, not wanting to
spoil it:*

**PA**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "So you stay when someone says no.")*
Donc tu restes quand on te dit non.

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "No. I stay when someone says no and is still there the next morning.")*
Non. Je reste quand quelqu'un dit non et qu'il est encore là le lendemain matin.

**PA**
*(French; SUBTITLED, a little lost: "That isn't the same as 'stay.'")*
Ce n'est pas la même chose que « reste ».

*Tyler looks at him a moment. It is the first sign of the day that a sentence was not quite what
he meant to say.*

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED, gently: "No. It's the reason.")*
Non. C'est la raison.

*Hana hands the rig back to Cam over the cloth, a clean pass, the lens never leaving the table.*

*The Duck has been waiting. It has been waiting the whole shot, with the soup spoon laid across its
knee like a sword, for exactly this lull. It clears its throat.*

**DUCK**
*(English)*
I told the factory I have telekinesis. I want to be clear that was true.

*The unit turns. Cam lets the lens drift to it. A duck in a tuxedo on a folding chair, deadly
serious.*

**DUCK (CONT'D)**
*(English)*
Nobody believes me. That's usually the bit.

*It looks at the salt cellar in the middle of the cloth. It does not touch it. The salt cellar
rises an inch off the cloth, hangs there, and drifts across the table in a slow, level line to
the seventh chair, where it descends and settles, neat, beside Tyler's bowl.*

*Nobody speaks. The shot, by long habit, holds. The Producer's steam, on the fourth chair, has stopped
moving.*

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED, mildly: "Thank you.")*
Merci.

**PA**
*(French; SUBTITLED, barely: "We believe you.")*
On te croit.

*The Duck does not answer at once. We hold on its face, which is a duck's face and so does very
little, and we watch it do very little with great precision.*

**DUCK**
*(English, after a time)*
That's a lot of people believing me at once.

**CAM (O.S.)**
*(French; SUBTITLED, behind the lens: "Get used to it.")*
Habitue-toi.

*The camera comes down. Not off. Cam sets the rig on the cloth, between the salt and the bread,
lens up. It looks at the tubes. The red light is on. The shot is still going.*
