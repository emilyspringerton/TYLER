**PRODUCTION NOTE, READ BEFORE THE SCRIPT:**

Tuesday, the third, NO ENTRY. On the first Tuesday Tyler told a story (VH27); on the second he
borrowed a camera (VH34). On this one he is invited home by a duck.

**Source canon.** `episodes/x00_the_custody_of_a_duck.md` (found footage; Series X): Tyler on a bench,
a folded hoodie, the Duck on the armrest. *"You never told me your name." — "You never asked. You
told me about the frog instead."* The hoodie, STINKIES COMMISSAIRE, is carried "the way you carry
something you intend to hand off in person, not ship." *"To who."* — *"Whoever's still standing
here when I'm not."* This is where that line comes due, and the Duck asks the question properly.

**Source canon, the Duck:** `just_a_duck.md` (Jack's Factory #shorts): *"The duck is wearing a
tuxedo. 'I have the power of telekinesis.'… The tree is speaking French. 'I'm the keeper of the
universe's greatest secret.'"* **The tree is speaking French.** The Duck forgot the French when
it forgot the tree.

**Form.** The Duck's apartment, in black and white (VH11), entered through the equipment-closet
door; no V.O. For once the Duck does not narrate. There is no voice-over because the Duck is not
telling the story; it is in it.

**Names.** A name said is a gift (VH25). Two are exchanged. Neither is in this script.

**Not resolved:** what the tree's secret is (the Duck says it is the whole thing); the Duck's name
(never).

*Dawn, or the room's version of it. The unit is asleep in its chairs, the long breathing sleep of people
who have been at a table for seventeen days. Tyler is awake, as ever. He is in the seventh chair, a pencil in
his hand, the crossword on his knee, and the horse beside him. The Duck, in the sixth, is not asleep either.*

*It slides off its chair. It crosses the room, small, in tuxedo and bare feet, to the equipment closet at
the dead end of the racks, a door none of them has opened: gray steel, a hasp, a faded sign that says
ÉQUIPEMENT. It stops in front of it. It looks back at Tyler.*

**DUCK**
*(English, quietly)*
Come on.

*Tyler sets down the pencil. The horse lifts her head. Cam, awake behind the rig in the dark, lifts hers.
Nobody says anything. She holds up one hand with the thumb pressed to the edge of the lens hood and
makes a little gesture with it, at the Duck, and at Tyler: a small opening. Green. The Duck nods. It opens the
steel door.*

---

```
INT. THE DUCK'S APARTMENT — NIGHT — BLACK AND WHITE
```

*The room from VH11, the room from VH21: Venetian blinds throwing slats across a table. A desk lamp. A
single glass with an inch of something in it. The thirteen-page calendar lies squared on the table under
the lamp, its corner exactly at the corner of the table. On the wall behind, a rectangle of cleaner plaster
the size of thirteen pages, and a nail with nothing on it. A folding chair is leaning, closed, in the
corner.*

*The Duck comes in first. Tyler follows, and the door behind him is a bedroom door now, a plain one, a
door that was never a closet's. He shuts it.*

*No narration. The Duck walks to the bed and sits on the end of it. It does not sit like a detective.
It sits like a duck.*

**DUCK**
*(English)*
Sit.

*He sits on the folding chair, which he opens. It does not creak. The Duck looks at it.*

**DUCK (CONT'D)**
*(English)*
That's the inspector's.

**TYLER**
*(English)*
He gave it to you.

**DUCK**
*(English)*
He left it. It's not the same.

*A silence of the particular kind that falls between two people who are very comfortable and are
not going to be again. Through the blinds, the street light. The man across the road is out with his dog,
walking slowly, with the air of someone with nowhere else to be at this hour.*

**TYLER**
*(English, easy)*
He's been walking that dog since the eleventh.

**DUCK**
*(English)*
It's a Tuesday.

---

*The Duck looks at its feet, which do not reach the floor. It speaks like a bird making a
decision to say a thing it has been carrying for some time, which is to say all at once.*

**DUCK**
*(English)*
You never told me your name.

*Tyler looks at it. In the black-and-white room his face is pale and kind and extremely tired. He has
heard this sentence before. The room remembers.*

**TYLER**
*(English)*
You never asked. You told me about the frog instead.

**DUCK**
*(English)*
The frog's shy. Somebody has to carry that.

*Tyler laughs, once, quietly. The Duck does not.*

**DUCK (CONT'D)**
*(English)*
I'm asking.

*A long moment. Tyler leans forward on the chair, forearms on his knees, and says it softly, one word, not
to the room, to the bird.*

**LOWER THIRD (DOC UI), in the Duck's own channel, the type small, the format from a different decade:**
```
AUDIO: 1 NAME WITHHELD (BY WHOM: SEAT 7)
```

*The Duck sits very still. It repeats it, silently, with its beak, once, to hear where it goes. Then it
looks up.*

**DUCK**
*(English)*
Mine's —

**LOWER THIRD (DOC UI):**
```
AUDIO: 1 NAME WITHHELD (BY WHOM: THE DUCK)
```

*Tyler hears it. He nods slowly. He says it back, very gently, so that the bird can hear that it has been
said correctly.*

**DUCK (CONT'D)**
*(English, after a time)*
Nobody's said that since the factory.

**TYLER**
*(English)*
I know.

---

*Tyler reaches into the inside pocket of the canvas jacket and takes out a hoodie. Gray. Folded
small, in the fold of a map you have read before, a fleece ten ounces thick with two words in black on the back:
STINKIES. COMMISSAIRE. It is the hoodie from a factory in a transcript. He holds it in both hands.*

**DUCK**
*(English)*
You're going to give that away.

**TYLER**
*(English)*
Eventually. That's what it's for.

**DUCK**
*(English)*
To who?

**TYLER**
*(English)*
Whoever's still standing here when I'm not.

*The Duck looks at the hoodie for a long time.*

**DUCK**
*(English)*
I'm sitting.

**TYLER**
*(English, mildly)*
Then it isn't due.

*He folds the hoodie once more along the line that was already there. He sets it on his knee. The Duck, on
the end of the bed, watches the hoodie, and then Tyler.*

**DUCK**
*(English, very evenly)*
When are you leaving?

*Tyler does not look away. He is not performing anything. He has put down, for the length of this exchange,
even the smile.*

**TYLER**
*(English)*
The twenty-second.

**DUCK**
*(English)*
I know. The tree told me.

---

**TYLER**
*(English)*
Which tree?

**DUCK**
*(English, with a slight air of explaining the obvious)*
The one in the factory. It speaks French. It was how I knew the language. When I stopped listening to
it, I forgot the French. I thought I'd forgotten the French. I'd forgotten the tree.

*Tyler is leaning forward. He has not been this interested in anything in seventeen days.*

**TYLER**
*(English)*
What does it say?

**DUCK**
*(English)*
It says bring milk.

*It indicates, with its wing, the white thermos on the nightstand. Tyler had not seen it. It is a plain
white enamel thermos, the kind you take on a train, dented at the base, a thin line of steam standing from
its cap.*

**TYLER**
*(English, slowly)*
Why milk?

**DUCK**
*(English)*
For a man who hasn't slept in a while.

*Tyler is silent. He looks at the thermos. He does not take it.*

**TYLER**
*(English)*
Not yet. I'd miss it.

**DUCK**
*(English, a little too pleased)*
That's what the tree said you'd say.

**TYLER**
*(English, laughing now, honestly)*
What's the secret? It's got a secret. It said so in the transcript. "The keeper of the universe's greatest
secret."

**DUCK**
*(English, gravely)*
Bring milk.

**TYLER**
*(English)*
That's it?

**DUCK**
*(English)*
The tree says it's most of it.

---

*They sit. The lamp. The street light. The man with the dog, across the road, is looking up at the window.
The Duck gets off the bed and goes to it, and parts the blinds with two feathers.*

**DUCK**
*(English, not turning)*
Take me with you.

**TYLER**
*(English)*
You're the one who stays.

**DUCK**
*(English)*
I'm sitting.

**TYLER**
*(English, rising, holding out the folding chair as if for someone to sit in it)*
Then stand up.

*The Duck looks at the chair. It does not sit in it. It stands on the carpet beside it, in the slats of light,
the smallest person in the room, and it straightens. It is a straightening you could measure on a
doorframe.*

*Across the road the man with the dog lifts a hand. The Duck waves one wing at him. He waves back.*

**LOWER THIRD (DOC UI), final card, in the Duck's channel:**
```
NO ENTRY
TWO NAMES (NOT WRITTEN)
ONE HOODIE (NOT DUE)
ONE THERMOS (MILK)
THE DUCK: STANDING
```
