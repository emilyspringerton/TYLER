**PRODUCTION NOTE, READ BEFORE THE SCRIPT:**

Thursday is Jupiter's day (Stolas #36, 7.83 Hz, *deep time sight*; Tyler's hum; `CLAUDE.md` Key
Frequencies). It is also the day the `JEUDI` battery is due. VH04: "it is not Thursday. It is not
due." Today it is Thursday. It is due. The Auditor has come to collect it, and that is the first
thing he does, and the last thing he does is count to twenty-one.

**Form.** The episode's middle is eleven numbered seconds. This is the **Hybrid Arc** at work
(`README.md` §XXIII–XXIV): *timeline sight*, the involuntary perception of adjacent branches as an
interference pattern, which in the series bible lasts eleven seconds and which Tyler "does not yet
control." The Doc UI numbers each second. The script prints each.

**Guard-rails, specifically:**
- **Rule 21:** the transit stop is not shown and not named. Tyler says "once" and hums.
- **Rule 20:** Tyler cannot explain the state in a sentence about himself; he describes only what
  he saw, about *them*.
- **Rule 19:** the Emily OS file is not mentioned. The room, once, tightens (UI SPACING:
  TIGHTENED, one second, at second six). No one remarks on it. Describe the room, not her.
- Timeline sight is **involuntary** this episode. He is mid-sentence, not mid-ritual, and he is as
  surprised as anyone.

**The eighth chair** is set on the PA's tape: half in the room, half in the doorway. Whose it is
isn't answered; the Auditor says "somebody always turns up," and a voice in the corridor says
*thank you.*

**Not resolved:** whether the glimpses are branches or warnings; the tenth ingredient; the sheet.

*Three raps on the air beside the corridor door. The Auditor comes in sideways with a folding chair
under each arm, which is one more than he has ever carried, and a lunchbox on a strap across his
chest. The steel lunchbox. The rig. The handwritten label written over so many times it is mostly
ink. It is Thursday. He is, if this is possible, happier than on Sunday.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French, his genders as ever wrong; SUBTITLED: "Good morning! I've come for my battery.")*
Bonjour ! Je suis venu chercher ma pile.

*No one moves. The sound guy has the Gap Meter in both hands, the brass warm. Cam is standing. She
has her hand over the pocket where she has kept the empty battery sleeve since the day without a date, and
for a second the whole unit does the arithmetic of what a battery is.*

**SOUND GUY**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "It's running the meter.")*
Elle fait marcher l'appareil.

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "I know. That's what it's for.")*
Je sais. C'est pour ça qu'elle sert.

*He holds out his hand. The sound guy opens the back of the Gap Meter and the dial swings off its
pin and falls to the stop. He gives up the cell, black and squat, the masking tape, the one word in
ballpoint. The Auditor sets it in the lunchbox. The lunchbox's needle sweeps. Settles. He nods.*

**THE AUDITOR (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Ninety-one percent. Good. Renewed until next Thursday.")*
Quatre-vingt-onze pour cent. Bien. Renouvelée jusqu'à jeudi prochain.

*He puts it back into the meter with a small click. The dial wakes. He nods again, a man closing a
file he enjoys.*

**THE AUDITOR (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "A thing that is due every Thursday is a thing that comes back every Thursday.
That isn't a loan. It's an appointment.")*
Une chose qui est due tous les jeudis est une chose qui revient tous les jeudis. Ce n'est pas un
prêt. C'est un rendez-vous.

*Cam takes her hand off her pocket.*

---

*He sets the chairs. He sets the first on the PA's tape, in front of the seventh chair, exactly
on the strip of silver, so that its front legs stand in the room and its back legs stand in the
doorway. The tape has a hoofprint in it, and a chair-leg print now. He squares it with the side of
his shoe.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "The list is seven. Somebody always turns up.")*
La liste fait sept. Il y a toujours quelqu'un qui arrive.

**OLDER HANA (O.S., corridor)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Thank you.")*
Merci.

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French, not turning; SUBTITLED: "Don't mention it, madam.")*
Je vous en prie, madame.

*The second chair he holds a moment, and puts down nowhere. He takes it back under his arm as a man
takes an umbrella he has decided to bring.*

---

*The Gap Meter, back on the equipment case, reads 3.98. The Auditor glances at it. He looks again.
He puts his glasses down his nose, the way a man does for a bill.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "It's drifting.")*
Il dérive.

**SOUND GUY**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Since Sunday. About a hertz a day.")*
Depuis dimanche. À peu près un hertz par jour.

*The Auditor looks at the needle a little longer than a man looks at a thing he is only
interested in. Then at Tyler, who is in the seventh chair, doing a crossword in the back of
somebody's logbook, with a pencil, and humming in two time signatures at once.*

**THE AUDITOR**
*(French, half to himself; SUBTITLED: "Twenty point eight seven, divided by —")*
Vingt virgule quatre-vingt-sept, divisé par —

*He stops. He takes the glasses off.*

**THE AUDITOR (CONT'D)**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "I'd rather not do arithmetic on a guest.")*
Je préfère ne pas faire d'arithmétique sur un invité.

*The sound guy has already done it. It takes him a second and a half. He says the number the way a
man says the time on a clock that has just stopped.*

**SOUND GUY**
*(French; SUBTITLED, quietly: "Twenty-one.")*
Vingt et un.

*The PA's pencil stops. The room does not go silent. The tubes go on breathing, the horse goes on
standing, the Producer's steam goes on standing from the mouth of the thermos on the fourth chair.
But there is a sense of a number having been put down on the cloth among the bread and the salt.*

*Tyler does not look up from the crossword.*

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED, mild, not looking up: "Seven across. Nine letters. 'Stays.'")*
Sept horizontal. Neuf lettres. « Reste. »

*No one answers. Hana, after a moment, in a voice she has to lower to hold steady:*

**HANA**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "'Remains.'")*
« Demeure. »

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "That fits.")*
Ça rentre.

*Afternoon. The Auditor has gone. The tubes are low. The hum, which on a Tuesday could not be
heard by any instrument in the building, is a physical presence in the room: on the sound guy's
dial it stands at 7.83 and does not waver, and under it, in the place where on any other day
there is a gap, there is a second line, coiled, that rises and falls and does not resolve.*

*Tyler is in the middle of a sentence.*

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "What I mean is that if you take the bread out of the —")*

have gone somewhere about nine inches behind the room.*

*Tyler blinks. He is looking at the PA, whose hand is on his arm. The sentence he was in the middle
of is not completed and will not be. He sits very still for a moment, and then he begins to hum.
Not 7/8. Not 11/8. The two of them, at once, in a ratio that does not come out even.*

**HANA**
*(French; SUBTITLED, low: "Has that happened before?")*
Ça t'est déjà arrivé ?

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Once.")*
Une fois.

*He does not elaborate. No one asks him to. The horse has come to stand against his shoulder, and
he puts his hand on her neck.*

**SOUND GUY**
*(French; SUBTITLED, barely: "Eleven seconds.")*
Onze secondes.

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED, with a small, honest attempt at a smile: "I didn't do that on purpose.")*
Je ne l'ai pas fait exprès.

**CAM**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "What did you see?")*
Qu'est-ce que tu as vu ?

*He thinks about it. He says the thing that is true, and that is about them, and that will be
misunderstood.*

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Somewhere there's a room where you're all very old. It's the nicest one.")*
Quelque part il y a une pièce où vous êtes tous très vieux. C'est la plus agréable.

*The PA's pencil moves. He writes: *très vieux.* He writes it the way one writes an address.*

**PA**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "Where are we in that one?")*
Où est-ce qu'on est, dans celle-là ?

**TYLER**
*(French; SUBTITLED: "The same chairs.")*
Dans les mêmes chaises.
