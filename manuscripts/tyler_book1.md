## CHAPTER 1 — "THE VOICE MESSAGE"

---

The garage doesn't let them leave right away.

Not physically — the door is right there, Mara can see it, an ordinary rectangle of cold Osaka night on the other side of it. But nobody moves toward it. VOICE MESSAGE (1) sits in the air where the call used to be, smaller now, patient in a way the call never was.

Hana is the one who finally reaches for it. Not Mara.

"If it wanted us moving through the Foxbody pad," Hana says, "it would've hidden the message until we picked a lane. It's showing us this because it thinks the message *is* a lane."

"So don't play it," Len says.

"We're going to play it." Hana's already reaching. "I want to know what shape the trap is before I decide whether I'm in it."

The message plays without a device, the same way the call arrived without one — a voice in the room, in the air, in the system.

It isn't the Promptor.

It's older. Rougher. Recorded, not rendered — the kind of tape hiss that means analog, means real, means somewhere a physical reel actually turned.

**VOICE (RECORDING)**

*"— tapes. Incredible tapes. People are talking about it. They're saying, 'how did you know,' and I tell them, I just have a feel for these things —"*

The recording cuts. Silence. Then, colder, in a completely different register — a system voice, not a human one:

**EMILY OS (RECORDING, OVERLAY)**

*"◈ LOG EXTRACTION ATTAINED. TARGET: TRUMP, D. (INVOLUNTARY / UNRESOLVED). METADATA SPIKE: 1978 CONVERGENCE EVENT."*

Jules actually drops the boom mic.

"That's not us," Jules says. "That's not our feed. That's — where did that come from, that's *archived*, that's not live —"

"February 15, 1978," Hana says. She isn't asking. "Caesars Palace sportsbook. Spinks-Ali fight."

Mara looks at her. "You know the date off the top of your head."

"I know it because it's Syndicate doctrine, not because I memorized a trivia card." Hana's voice has gone flat, procedural — the voice she uses when she's quoting something instead of feeling it. "Vassago #42. Eleven-point-eleven hertz. Soft precognition. It's the frequency the Syndicate uses for tracking unanchored migrants — people who get pulled loose from their own timeline without ever finding out it happened to them. He's the textbook case. Sportsbook glitches for a second, shows him a string of numbers instead of odds, and by the time it clears he's had seventy-two minutes he doesn't remember having. He walks out thinking he had a very good meeting about marble."

"That's insane," Len says.

"It's not insane, it's efficient." Hana's already pacing, already building the shape of it out loud the way she built the ignition-order trap out loud twenty minutes ago. "An aware migrant fights the system. Tries to report it, tries to prove it, generates paperwork the Syndicate then has to bury. An *unaware* migrant generates zero resistance. He doesn't know he moved, so there's nothing to suppress. He's not hiding from anyone. He's the perfect blind spot — a real, loud, documented person standing in the middle of a real, loud, documented room, and none of the recording equipment in a three-block radius can hold a clean signal while he's in it."

"That's not a compliment," Mara says.

"It's not supposed to be. It's a *function*. The doctrine's own words, not mine: he's accidentally Tyler's best cover. Every minute Trump's timeline is smeared across a sportsbook floor is a minute nobody's instruments can find whatever else is moving through the same city that night."

She looks up.

"1978 wasn't a quiet year to be tracking anything. Camp David happens the same September — real reconciliation, real Amon activation, the biggest one the whole century logs. Three different men sit in the Chair of Peter in seventy-one days that same fall, and the last one breaks four hundred and fifty-six years of Italian succession on the way in. The processor architecture the entire modern world still runs on gets etched into silicon that spring. It's a loud year. It's the loudest year in the whole archive, maybe. And somewhere in the middle of all of it, completely by accident, a man who doesn't know he's slipping through time is generating exactly enough noise to make sure nobody's listening equipment can get a clean fix on anything else moving through 1978 at the same time."

Len, quietly: "Including us."

"Including whatever's actually worth finding in 1978," Hana says. "We don't know yet if that's us."

---

Mara is still looking at the overlay, at the frozen system log hanging in the air.

*TARGET IS RE-WRITING THE LEDGER BY EXISTING.*

*THIS BUILD IS INCREASINGLY NOISY.*

"He'll never know any of this happened to him," she says.

"That's correct," Hana says. "That's the whole point. It's not a punishment. It's not even really about him. He's load-bearing the same way a wall is load-bearing — nobody designed the wall to *mean* anything, it's just standing in the one place that keeps the rest of the structure from coming down. Pull it out and you don't get justice. You get a building that falls down on people who had nothing to do with the wall."

"So we leave him in it."

"We leave him in it." Hana kills the recording with a flat, decisive gesture at nothing — the air where the overlay was. "Same as the Syndicate has for four decades. Same as they will for however many more it takes. The abacus keeps clicking. He keeps having a very good real estate meeting. Nobody tells him."

## OUT — THE SECOND MESSAGE

---

The system isn't quite done.

A second line resolves under the first, later than the rest, like it had to wait its turn.

```
◈ EMILY OS: ARCHIVE NOTE.
  1978 IS FULLY ALLOCATED.
  DO NOT REQUEST ADDITIONAL BANDWIDTH FROM THIS YEAR.
  IT IS ALREADY HOLDING MORE THAN IT WAS BUILT TO HOLD.
```

Nobody in the garage says anything for a moment.

Then Mara, quiet, almost to herself:

"You know I hate calls."

Hana, without looking up from the dead overlay:

"I know. That's why you didn't take it. Take a message from the ones you're not sure you can survive answering."

The garage lights come back up slowly, the way they'd dimmed — not a switch, a decision.

The Foxbody pad is still glowing. STATUS: READY. ACTION: ENTER.

Nobody moves toward it yet.

*END CHAPTER 1.*
