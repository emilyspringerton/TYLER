## CHAPTER 1 — "YOU KNOW I HATE CALLS"

---

The Osaka Garage hums like it's thinking.

Not loud—steady.

A machine room heartbeat that doesn't care how scared you are.

Mara stands at the threshold with the tripod still on her shoulder, snow melting into her hoodie and then vanishing as if the garage refuses to admit weather exists.

Hana is inside already, eyes scanning corners, seams, the overlaps in the walls—the double shell. She notices the way the ceiling overlaps itself like layered armor.

A place built by someone who has also fallen into voids.

Len stares at the portal frame like a man seeing a check with his name on it.

Jules holds the boom mic up, but the mic feels stupid here—sound in this space has a cleaner edge, like it's been noise-gated by the architecture.

The prompt floats in the air:

IGNITION ORDER REQUIRED

BLACK → GREEN → RED → YELLOW → WHITE

Mara's throat is dry.

Hana's voice is tight.

HANA

Don't touch it.

Mara doesn't answer.

She doesn't move.

Because she can feel what happens if she does.

And the worst part is: she can feel what happens if she doesn't.

The garage waits.

It's polite in the way that traps are polite.

Then—softly—something rings.

Not a phone.

Not a bell.

A sound like an old flip phone vibrating on a table inside a closed drawer.

Once.

Then again.

And then the overlay appears, crisp and cruel:

INCOMING CALL

Mara's stomach drops.

She looks around for a device.

There isn't one.

The "call" is in the room.

In the air.

In the system.

A second line appears underneath:

CALLER: PROMPTOR

Hana's face twists like she tasted something rotten.

Len lets out a little sound—half laugh, half panic.

LEN

Oh my God.

Mara's hands go cold.

MARA

No.

The call overlay blinks like a heartbeat.

ACCEPT

DECLINE

Mara feels the trap in the shape of the buttons.

If she declines, it's an input.

If she accepts, it's an input.

Refusal is still data.

The prompt doesn't care.

It just wants your fingers on something.

Hana steps between Mara and the floating UI like she can physically block a concept.

Her voice is sharp, low.

HANA

You know I hate calls.

Mara blinks.

For a second it feels absurd—like a normal complaint you'd make about a coworker.

Then it hits: Hana isn't saying it as a joke.

She's saying it like a boundary she's had to defend her whole life.

Calls are unrecorded conversations that still leave consequences.

Calls are where people manipulate you.

Calls are where you can't screenshot the lie.

The call overlay continues to blink.

Jules whispers into comms, frightened.

JULES (comms)

There's no device. It's… it's just—

MARA

Yeah. I know.

Len steps forward, producer brain trying to make it profitable.

LEN

If we take it, we'll know what he wants.

Hana snaps.

HANA

He wants you to take it.

Len freezes.

Hana's eyes are hard.

HANA

He wants to move you from scene to scene without resistance.

Mara's mind flashes: SLIDE_SLIDE.

The way the world reframed itself without asking.

The way the "REC" overlay tried to bloom on its own.

The way the garage door was labeled like a file path.

Mara feels the tripod shift on her shoulder.

A tool.

A weapon.

A leash.

The call overlay pulses again, and this time a new line appears, like the system is trying to sweeten the deal:

REWARD: STABILITY

Hana's laugh is short and bitter.

HANA

Stability is a disease.

The call pulses.

REWARD: CONTROL

Mara's eyes flick to the vehicles—Foxbody '93, landship, tarped silhouettes.

Control in the form of horsepower.

Control in the form of thick walls.

Control in the form of a portal that says "VS0" like an invitation.

The call pulses one more time, and the garage itself seems to lean in:

REWARD: ANSWERS ABOUT PRINCESS

Mara inhales sharply.

Hana goes still.

So still that for a second she looks like she might vanish.

Mara's voice is barely there.

MARA

Hana…

Hana's jaw clenches so hard the tendons show.

HANA

Don't.

Mara's hands tighten.

Because Princess is the fracture.

Princess is the thing Hana refuses to let be indexed.

The Promptor is offering it like bait.

And something in Mara—the director, the doctor, the thief of cookies, the woman with a birthday she can't enjoy—wants to grab it.

Because curiosity is a form of hunger.

And hunger makes you sloppy.

The call overlay flickers.

ACCEPT

DECLINE

Mara doesn't touch either.

She shifts the tripod off her shoulder, plants it on the floor—firm, deliberate.

Not filming.

Just placing weight.

The garage hum changes slightly, like it's surprised by her choice.

Hana watches, wary.

Mara looks at Hana.

MARA

If I take the call, it owns me.

Hana's eyes flash—approval, fear, something else.

HANA

If you decline, it owns you too.

Mara nods, grim.

MARA

So we do neither.

Len's eyebrows lift.

LEN

That's not—

Hana cuts him off.

HANA

That's exactly.

Mara steps past Hana and toward the ignition order prompt instead—because if the system is demanding an input, you can sometimes survive by choosing the input you want to give.

Her eyes track the foxes.

Black, Green, Red, Yellow, White.

They're watching like keys waiting to be turned.

Mara raises a hand.

Not to accept.

Not to decline.

She points at the garage wall—thick, overlapping.

A safe room.

And she speaks, present tense, like a spell.

MARA

I'm choosing the garage.

The call overlay stutters.

The ACCEPT/DECLINE buttons blur, as if the UI didn't anticipate a third option.

Hana's voice is a whisper, shocked.

HANA

You can do that?

Mara doesn't know.

She's just doing it anyway.

The call overlay collapses into a single line:

CALL MISSED

Then, immediately:

VOICE MESSAGE (1)

Mara's stomach drops.

Hana's eyes narrow.

Len makes a tiny excited noise he can't help.

Jules' mic spikes again, catching that faint clicking rhythm under the concrete—flip-phone texting.

And then the garage lights dim, except for one pad on the floor that ignites with a soft glow under the Foxbody '93.

A label appears above it in the second overlay environment—clean, OS-like:

VEHICLE PAD: FOXBODY_93

STATUS: READY

ACTION: ENTER

Mara turns to Hana.

MARA

He's trying to route us through tools instead of words.

Hana nods once, tight.

HANA

Good. Tools leave fingerprints.

Mara looks back at the Foxbody. At the portal frame. At the thick walls.

The garage is giving them choices now, but they aren't real choices.

They're lanes.

Fox lanes.

Predictable movement.

A hunt corridor disguised as a hub.

Mara looks at her tripod.

At the camera man she didn't ask to become.

She mutters, almost to herself:

MARA

You know I hate calls.

Hana's mouth twitches—almost a smile, almost grief.

HANA

Then don't answer.

Mara takes a step toward the Foxbody pad.

The foxes' ears tilt.

The portal hum deepens.

The garage overlays a final line—quiet, dangerous:

G_REGULATORS: MODE SELECT

GARAGE / STADIUM / RECORDER

Mara's heart pounds.

Because the system isn't asking what she wants.

It's asking what she's willing to be.

CUT TO BLACK.
