## CHAPTER 1 — "THE IGNITION ORDER"

---

The gymnasium doesn't move. That's the part that gets Mara, later, replaying it in her head at three in the morning — not the voice in the walls, not the frozen crew, but the fact that a full basketball court with bleachers and category banners just sits there, completely still, while a dead flip phone in a painted circle decides everyone's next forty seconds.

Tyler holds the phone against his ear like it might bite him if he loosens his grip.

**SUIT (V.O.)**
I'm the part of you that remembers the ignition order.

**TYLER**
That's not an answer. That's a riddle wearing an answer's clothes.

**SUIT (V.O.)**
*(amused, unbothered)*
Most true things are, the first time you hear them.

Hana's stillness has a different quality now than it did thirty seconds ago. Before, it was procedural — a woman running a desk, running a building, running a conversation she's had a hundred times with a hundred different Subjects. Now it's the stillness of someone listening to a frequency she recognizes and had hoped never to hear again.

**HANA**
*(quiet, to no one, almost to herself)*
He shouldn't have that number.

**TYLER**
*(into the phone)*
She says you shouldn't have this number.

**SUIT (V.O.)**
She's not wrong. She's also not the one holding the phone.

Tyler looks at Hana. Hana looks at the phone like it's a wound that reopened on its own.

**TYLER**
What's the ignition order.

A pause on the line. Long enough that Tyler pulls it slightly away from his ear to check it's still connected. It is. The pause was deliberate.

**SUIT (V.O.)**
Black. Green. Red. Yellow. White.

Something in the gymnasium's lighting rig flickers — not dramatically, just once, like a held breath being let out through the nose. Nobody but Jules notices. Jules always notices the wrong thing at the right time; it's the whole reason Mara keeps him on sound.

**JULES**
*(barely a whisper, to Mara)*
That's — that's a real sequence. That's not random. Somebody built that on purpose.

**MARA**
*(still whispering, still directing even now)*
Get the banners in frame. EVIDENCE, BLAME, CUSTODY, APPEAL. Wide, then a slow push on the circle.

**LEN**
*(whisper, half-panicked, half-professional)*
Mara, I don't think this is the segment where we cut to B-roll.

**MARA**
Every segment is the segment where we cut to B-roll, Len. That's the job.

Tyler, still holding the phone, still watching Hana's face for a tell she isn't giving him:

**TYLER**
What happens after white.

**SUIT (V.O.)**
After white, you stop asking me and start asking her.

The line goes dead. Not a click — the specific, total silence of a call that was never really occupying a channel to begin with. Tyler lowers the phone slowly. It's just a scuffed old flip phone again, closed screen, no bars, no battery indicator, nothing.

He looks up at Hana.

**TYLER**
Ignition order. Black, green, red, yellow, white. What is it.

Hana doesn't answer right away. She walks to center court, crouches by the circle, and looks at the phone in Tyler's hand like it's something she used to own and gave up custody of a long time ago.

**HANA**
It's how you start something that isn't supposed to be started twice.

**TYLER**
That's not an answer either. Everyone in this building talks like a fortune cookie that went to law school.

For the first time since the desk, something that might genuinely be the beginning of a smile moves across Hana's face and doesn't finish arriving.

**HANA**
Good. You're paying attention to the shape of the sentences instead of just the words. That's Stage 3 behavior. You're ahead of schedule.

**MARA**
*(finding her voice again, sharper now)*
Ahead of whose schedule. Ours or yours?

Hana looks at her — really looks, the way she hasn't looked at any of the crew until now.

**HANA**
There's only one schedule, Director. You've just been allowed to think you were running it.

She stands. Straightens her cardigan. The clipboard is still on the desk back in the lobby, a hundred feet and one impossible hallway away, but she doesn't seem worried about that at all.

**HANA (CONT.)**
Black is refusal. Green is consent. Red is cost. Yellow is the part everyone forgets to plan for. White is what's left over once you've paid for all four.

**TYLER**
And if I never go through it?

**HANA**
Then you stay Unclaimed forever, and forever is a real, specific number of years in this file, not a figure of speech. I've watched people choose that. I'm not going to tell you it's the wrong choice. I'm going to tell you it's the *slower* one.

She checks something invisible — a watch that isn't on her wrist, a clock that isn't on the wall — and exhales, the first unguarded thing she's done since "You're late."

**HANA (CONT.)**
Black just started the moment you picked up that phone. You didn't consent to anything yet. You just stopped refusing to listen. That's the whole first stage. That's all it ever is.

**LEN**
*(whisper, to Mara)*
Did we just watch stage one happen on camera?

**MARA**
*(not whispering anymore)*
We watched stage one happen on camera whether we understood it or not. That's the only kind of footage that's ever mattered in this building.

Hana starts back toward the gymnasium doors — toward the hallway, toward the lobby, toward whatever comes after a scene like this one.

She stops at the threshold, the same way she stopped at the last one.

**HANA**
Green is next. Green needs a room with everyone in it who's going to be blamed if this goes wrong.

She looks, deliberately, at all four of them — Tyler, Mara, Len, Jules — one at a time, the way a teacher takes attendance on a day she already knows nobody's absent.

**HANA (CONT.)**
That's already true. It has been since you followed me past the fence.

*END CHAPTER 1.*
